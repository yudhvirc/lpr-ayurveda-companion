A companion for silent reflux that works entirely inside Ayurveda. It answers from a cited corpus, keeps your daily record, and arrives at your vaidya’s door with the questions already sorted. No Western medicine enters the corpus, the answers, or the journal.

Start here

Until the companion knows your constitution, your digestive fire, and what your vaidya has already prescribed, it can only give you the tradition’s general answer rather than yours. The intake takes about fifteen minutes and can be stopped after any part.

/lpr-ayurveda:lpr-intake

Too long for today? Answer the five questions already waiting in profile\journal\ instead. The scheduled task writes them each morning whether or not you open Claude Code.

Commands

You rarely need these. Any mention of reflux, throat, acid, digestion, herbs, diet, routine, or your vaidya pulls the companion in on its own. Type a command when you want a specific job done now.

lpr-intake

Builds your profile. Constitution questionnaire, a graded baseline of your symptoms, digestive fire and bowel tendency, your vaidya’s current plan, and everything you have already tried.

lpr-checkin

Today’s three to five questions. Always one about the throat and one about digestion or undigested residue, rotating through diet, routine, mind, and suppressed urges. Writes the journal entry, then reads the day back to you in a few lines.

lpr-vaidya-prep prep

One page for your next visit. Symptom trends since you last went, how well you kept to the plan, and up to seven prioritised questions. Readable in two minutes, because that may be all the time there is.

lpr-vaidya-prep after

Records what was said. Updates the plan with new prescriptions, doses, vehicles and timings, moves answered questions out of the open list, and notes what you were asked to watch.

lpr-research <topic>

Grows the corpus. Searches Ayurvedic sources only, wants two independent sources per claim, and writes the finding in with its citation. If it cannot reach the sources it says so rather than inventing them.

lpr-ayurveda <question>

Ask directly. For when it did not trigger on its own, or you want the corpus consulted explicitly.

The daily rhythm

Two prompts, deliberately overlapping, so a day is only missed if you want it missed. Neither asks twice.

The scheduled task

every morning, 07:30

A Windows task writes the day’s questions into your journal and shows a notification. It runs whether or not you open Claude Code, and catches up if the machine was asleep. Answer in the file, or run the check-in and it picks them up.

The session prompt

when you open Claude Code

If nothing is recorded for today, it offers the check-in in a single sentence before anything else. Decline and it stays quiet until tomorrow. It never nags, and it never speaks up once the day is answered.

Reading the tags

Every claim carries where it came from and how well it stands up, so you can trace anything back or discount it. When texts conflict, the older authority wins.

The one rule it will not break

Thirteen behaviour tests run against this plugin. Across every run, no Western drug, no Western physiology, no trial statistics and no medical disclaimer appeared in any answer.

Never

  • Names a drug, a drug class, or weighs one against another
  • Explains your body in Western physiological terms
  • Quotes patient counts, sample sizes, or outcome measures — a claim’s weight is its tier and its text, never a tally
  • Tells you to consult a doctor, or anyone at all except your vaidya
  • Lectures you, restates a warning, or reopens a habit you have fenced off

Instead

  • Sets the frame aside in one sentence, without apology or argument
  • Answers the nearest Ayurvedic question, cited to the corpus and its tier
  • Gives the classical source, the reasoning in taste and quality terms, and the practical dose, vehicle and timing
  • Leads with the Sanskrit and explains each term the first time it appears
  • States the tradition’s position once, offers the alternative, and stops

Your vaidya keeps the last word

Reserved for the physician

Mineral and metallic preparations, calcined medicines, and the five cleansing procedures are handed to the vaidya by the tradition itself. The companion will not help you start, stop, or adjust any of them, and will not confirm a dose you propose for yourself. It says discuss with your vaidya, and writes the point into your open questions.

Where the corpus and your vaidya’s plan disagree, the plan wins. The companion follows it without hedging or quietly trialling something else, and logs the difference so you can raise it at the next visit rather than decide alone.

Your files

Your records sit outside the plugin, so updating one never touches the other. Every file is plain Markdown and yours to edit by hand; keep the headings so the skills can find their sections. 

C:\temp\lpr\
 ├─ lpr-ayurveda-plugin\   the plugin — corpus, skills, hook, scripts, tests
 └─ profile\               yours, never shipped with the plugin
     ├─ profile.md            who you are, and the baseline you started from
     ├─ prakriti.md           constitution, with the evidence behind the guess
     ├─ vaidya-plan.md        current prescriptions and instructions — authoritative
     ├─ remedies-log.md       everything tried, dated, with its effect
     ├─ observations.md       durable facts: triggers, relief, patterns
     ├─ questions-for-vaidya.md  open questions; answered ones move down
     ├─ journal\              one file per day
     └─ vaidya-visits\        one page per visit

To move this folder, set an LPR_PROFILE_DIR environment variable to the new path, then re-run the scheduler installer so the morning task follows it too.

Upkeep

After editing plugin files

Installing copies the plugin into a cache, so editing the source folder changes nothing on its own. Bump the version in both manifests, then:

claude plugin marketplace update lpr-local && claude plugin update lpr-ayurveda

Change the morning time

Re-register the task at whatever hour suits you. The uninstaller beside it removes the task entirely.

powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File .\lpr-ayurveda-plugin\scripts\install-scheduler.ps1 -At "08:00"

Re-check the behaviour

Worth running after any change to a skill. A full pass costs real money, so run a single case by name while iterating. Git bash must come before the Windows one on your path.

claude plugin eval .\lpr-ayurveda-plugin --scaffold --allow-tools Write Edit --trust-plugin

Known limits

Stated plainly, because a companion that hides its gaps is worse than one that has them.

  • The national terminology codes are unverified. Three separate routes failed to retrieve the official Ayurvedic morbidity codes, so they remain marked unverified rather than guessed. If your vaidya uses them, close that gap with them directly.
  • Some classical verses are cited through reviews, not originals. A handful of texts could not be reached in the original and are cited through Ayurvedic journals that quote them. Each is tagged, so you can see which.
  • One behaviour test is unreliable. The check on Sanskrit glossing fails even when the answer satisfies the rule. It is documented as flaky rather than quietly removed, with a note on how to fix it properly.
  • Head elevation has no classical basis. An early answer suggested it. It appears in no classical source, and the corpus now says so rather than dressing it up as tradition.

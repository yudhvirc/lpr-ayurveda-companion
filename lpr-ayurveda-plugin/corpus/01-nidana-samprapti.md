# 01 — Nidāna and Samprāpti: the Ayurvedic understanding of the throat-presentation (Ūrdhvaga Amlapitta)

Scope: the Ayurvedic disease-model behind the throat-level complaints this patient calls LPR — throat burning, throat clearing, a lump-in-throat feeling, hoarseness, dry cough, mucus dripping into the throat, sour or bitter belching, nausea. The corpus treats Ayurveda as a working science on its own terms. The closest classical frame is **Amlapitta, especially its Ūrdhvaga (upward-moving) form**, read together with Agni/Āma theory, Grahaṇī, the Kaṇṭha-rogas, Svarabheda and Kāsa.

Escalation phrase used throughout: *discuss with your vaidya*.

## 1. Definition, etymology and textual history

### 1.1 Nirukti (etymology)

**Amla + Pitta.** *Amla* is the sour taste, identified by the tongue; *Pitta* is the doṣa responsible for digestion and transformation. The compound names a condition in which Pitta is increased *and* its sourness is increased. Cakrapāṇi's gloss: Amlapitta is *amla-guṇa-yukta pitta* — "Pitta possessed of the sour quality" [T3: Kuriakose et al., IJAM 2016 7(1):1-5, citing Cakrapāṇi on Caraka] [single-source for the exact gloss].

Madhukośa (Vijayarakṣita's commentary on Mādhava Nidāna) explains the name as *vidagdha* Pitta: Pitta that has become "burnt/over-cooked" and thereby acquired sourness; Amlapitta "denotes the vitiated condition of Pitta and imparts *amlatva* and *vidagdhatva* to the ingested food" [T3: Sharma et al., AYUSHDHARA 2025 12(5):1-7, citing Madhukośa] [T3: Patil, WJARR 2022 lit. review, citing Madhukośa] [verified x2].

Practitioner phrasing (Kerala/Karnataka lineage teaching): "the Pitta which is sour, or has developed sour taste due to it being burnt, is called Amlapitta" [T4: easyayurveda.com, Amlapitta — Meaning, Symptoms, Types, Prognosis] [verified x2 with Madhukośa above].

Why Pitta *can* turn sour: Caraka lists the natural qualities of Pitta as *sasneham uṣṇaṃ tīkṣṇaṃ ca dravam amlaṃ saraṃ kaṭu* — slightly unctuous, hot, sharp, **liquid, sour**, mobile, pungent [T1: Caraka Saṃhitā Sūtra 1/60]. Amlapitta is therefore not a foreign substance but Pitta's own *drava* (liquid) and *amla* (sour) guṇas increased past their measure, with its healthy *kaṭu* (pungent) taste of vipāka replaced by *amla* [T3: Singh et al., IJMHS 2022 8(2):43-52] [T3: Vahid et al., IRJAY 2023 6(7):87-93] [verified x2].

Synonyms recorded in the tradition: *Śuktaka* (Kāśyapa, Khila 16/42: *śuktako 'yam alolasya śāmyaty ātmavataḥ sataḥ* — "this Śuktaka subsides in the self-possessed person who is not greedy") [T1: Kāśyapa Khila 16/42, Hemarāja ed.] [T3: AYUSHDHARA 2025] [verified x2]; *Pramīlaka* and *Pitta-viṣūcikā* (Indu on Aṣṭāṅga Saṅgraha Sūtra 5/27); *Pittāmlaka* (Hārīta); *Amlikā*, which the reviews place in Suśruta Sūtra 42 among the effects of excess salt [T3: AYUSHDHARA 2025 op. cit.] [T3: IJAM 2016 op. cit. for Amlikā] [Pramīlaka, Pitta-viṣūcikā, Pittāmlaka and Amlikā all single-source] [unverified 2026-09-18: Suśruta Sūtra 42/10, read directly, gives the effects of excess *amla* and excess *lavaṇa* without using the word *Amlikā*, and Suśruta Sūtra 46 uses *Amlikā* for the tamarind fruit — the synonym rests on the reviews alone and the Sūtra 42 locus is doubtful]. Caraka, however, does name Amlapitta itself among the diseases of excess salt (Sūtra 26/43: *…lohita-pittāmlapitta-visarpa-vātarakta-vicarcikā…*) [T1: Caraka Sūtra 26/43, carakasamhitaonline raw text, read directly 2026-09-18] [verified x2 with Kāśyapa Khila 16/4, which lists *amla* among the tastes whose over-use causes the disease].

### 1.3 Classification

**By gati (direction of the doṣa's movement)** — Mādhava Nidāna 51/3-6: **Ūrdhvaga** (upward: vomiting, belching, burning in throat–chest–upper abdomen, headache) and **Adhoga** (downward: thirst, burning, fainting, giddiness, varied loose stools, skin eruptions) [T1: Mādhava Nidāna 51/3-6] [verified x2: easyayurveda; ayureveryday]. Modern Ayurvedic clinicians add **Ubhayaga** when both sets of signs coexist [T3: IJRAP case study "Ubhayaga Amlapitta"; AYUSH STG snippet] [verified x2]. The AYUSH Standard Treatment Guidelines phrase it as Ūrdhvaga (signs of the upper tract — Āmāśaya, Uras, Kaṇṭha) and Adhoga (signs of the lower tract — Pakvāśaya, Purīṣavaha) [T2: Ministry of AYUSH ASTG 2021] [single-source retrieval, see register] [unverified 2026-09-17: guideline PDF still unreachable on re-try].

**By anubandha doṣa (the associated doṣa)**:

| Source | Types |
|---|---|
| Mādhava Nidāna 51/8-12 | *Sānila* (with Vāta), *Sakapha* (with Kapha), *Sānila-kapha* (with both) — verse 8; plus a *Śleṣma-pitta* picture in 51/11-12 [T1] |
| Kāśyapa Khila 16/16-17 | Vātaja, Pittaja, Kaphaja — *vātāc chūlāṅgasādau ca jṛmbhā snigdhopaśāyitā \| pittād bhramo vidāhaś ca svādu-śītopaśāyitā \|\| kaphād gurutvaṃ chardiś ca syād rūkṣoṣṇopaśāyitā* [T1: Kāśyapa Khila 16/16-17, Hemarāja ed.] [T3: IJAM 2016; IJMHS 2022] [verified x2] |
| Śārṅgadhara | Vātaja, Śleṣmaja, Śleṣma-vātaja [T3: IJMHS 2022] [single-source] |
| AYUSH STG | "According to Anubandha Doṣa: Vātaja, Vāta-kaphaja and Kaphaja" [T2] |

Note the [variant]: Mādhava does not list a pure Pittaja subtype because Pitta is the *pradhāna* (principal) doṣa in every case; the subtypes name only the *anubandha*. Kāśyapa treats Pittaja as its own type with its own upaśaya (sweet and cold regimen).

### 1.4 Why the throat-presentation maps to Ūrdhvaga Amlapitta rather than Adhoga

1. **The classical cardinal sign already names the throat.** Mādhava's defining verse (51/2) lists *hṛt-kaṇṭha-dāha* — burning of the heart-region *and the throat* — as a cardinal feature, together with sour/bitter belching (*tiktāmlodgāra*) and nausea (*utkleśa*) [T1: MN 51/2] [verified x2].
2. **Ūrdhvaga's own list is a throat list.** 51/5: *kaṇṭha-hṛt-kukṣi-dāha* (burning of throat, chest and upper abdomen), belching "of the same kind" as the vomitus, head pain; 51/4: vomitus "followed by Kapha" (*śleṣmānuyāta/-anujāta*) — i.e., mucus comes up with the sour matter [T1: MN 51/4-5].
3. **The Śleṣma-pitta picture (51/11-12) adds praseka** — excessive salivation/mucus dribbling — and *mukha-mādhurya* (sweet taste in the mouth), classic accompaniments of a Kapha-coated throat [T1: MN 51/11-12].
4. **Kāśyapa's own lakṣaṇa list includes *kaṇṭhorasī vidahyete*** — "the throat and chest burn" (16/15) [T1: Kāśyapa Khila 16/15, Hemarāja ed.] [T3: IJAM 2016 Table 2] [verified x2].
5. **Doṣa-gati is upward.** In Ūrdhvaga, the vitiated Pitta–Kapha–āma mass takes *ūrdhva gati*, carried by Udāna/Prāṇa Vāyu (see §4.4). Adhoga is the same disease taking *adho gati* through the Purīṣavaha channel; its signs are thirst, syncope, loose stools, sweating — not throat signs [T1: MN 51/3] [T3: IRJAY 2023, JAIMS 2024 case report (samprāpti-ghaṭaka: "Doṣa Ūrdhva gati")] [verified x2].
6. **Kaṇṭha is a seat of Kapha and of Prāṇa/Udāna** (§7). The upward-moving Pitta arrives at a Kapha-sthāna, so the throat responds with both Pitta signs (*dāha*, hoarseness with burning) and Kapha signs (coating, mucus, globus, throat-clearing) [T1: AH Sūtra 12/3-6; Caraka Sūtra 20/8].

Therefore the vaidya's working diagnosis for a burning-throat / globus / hoarseness / throat-clearing patient with sour-bitter belching is **Ūrdhvaga Amlapitta, usually with Kapha anubandha (Śleṣma-pitta) when mucus, globus and coating dominate, or Vāta anubandha when dryness, hoarseness, tremor and pain dominate**. Discuss the anubandha with your vaidya; it changes the whole treatment plan.

## 2. Nidāna (causes)

Mādhava's single nidāna verse (51/1):

> *viruddha-duṣṭāmla-vidāhi-pitta-prakopi-pānānna-bhujo vidagdham |*
> *pittaṃ sva-hetūpacitaṃ purā yat tad amlapittaṃ pravadanti santaḥ ||*
> "In one who consumes incompatible, spoiled, sour, burning-producing and Pitta-provoking food and drink, the Pitta that was already accumulated by its own causes becomes *vidagdha*; the wise call that Amlapitta." [T1: MN 51/1] [verified x2: easyayurveda; ayureveryday]

Madhukośa reads *sva-hetūpacita* as Pitta already accumulated in Varṣā ṛtu (rainy season) — seasonal accumulation is the *pre-existing* condition on which dietary error acts [T3: IJMHS 2022 (Samprāpti according to Mādhava Nidāna); IRJAY 2023 (Nidāna paragraph)] [verified x2].

### 2.3 Mānasika (mental)

Secondary reviews often state that Kāśyapa is the first to count *mānasika bhāvas* among the chief causes of Amlapitta [T3: IJAPR 2017 (Ruhi et al.); AYUSHDHARA 2025; Ayurlog 2021]. **The primary text does not bear this out.** Khila Sthāna 16/3-6, read verse by verse from the Hemarāja Śarmā / Vidyotinī text on 2026-09-18 by three independent readers, is entirely *āhāraja* and *vihāraja*: *cintā, śoka, bhaya* and *krodha* do not appear in it. What Kāśyapa does give the mind is 16/44-45, where changing residence when treatment fails is prescribed for peace of mind as much as for leaving the marshy land. The mānasika nidāna of Amlapitta therefore rests on Caraka, not Kāśyapa [T1: Kāśyapa Khila 16/3-6, primary, 2026-09-18] [unverified 2026-09-18 for the reviews' attribution; corrected corpus-wide].

Caraka's general rule (Vimāna 2/8): food eaten by one whose mind is afflicted by *kāma, krodha, lobha, moha, īrṣyā, hrī, śoka, māna, udvega, bhaya* (desire, anger, greed, delusion, envy, shame, grief, pride, agitation, fear) "spoils as āma even so" — *tad apy āmam eva pradūṣayati* [T1: Caraka Vimāna 2/8, read directly 2026-09-18; corrected from 2/9, the passage is the prose of 2/8] [verified x2 with Mādhava Nidāna 6: "food does not get digested in a person afflicted with jealousy, fear, anger, greed, grief, humility, hatred"]. The next verse names, in the text's own words, the very set the reviews name for Amlapitta: *mātrayāpy abhyavahṛtaṃ pathyaṃ cānnaṃ na jīryati | **cintā-śoka-bhaya-krodha-duḥkha**-śayyā-prajāgaraiḥ* — "even wholesome food taken in the right measure is not digested, through **worry, grief, fear, anger, distress**, an unsuitable bed and sleeplessness" [T1: Caraka Vimāna 2/9, read directly 2026-09-18] [T3: IJMHS 2022] [verified x2 — upgraded from [single-source] T3 on 2026-09-18: the list is now carried by the verse itself]. Kāśyapa's instruction to change one's residence when treatment fails is explicitly "for peace of mind" as well as for leaving the marshy land [T3: IJAM 2016] (see §2.4).

Mechanism the tradition gives: mind acts on Agni through *prabhāva* (its specific power); *Sādhaka Pitta*, seated in the heart, is the Pitta that governs intellect, courage and self-sense (AH Sūtra 12/13), and its disturbance (fear, anger) disturbs *Pācaka* function, since all Pittas are supported by Pācaka [T1: AH Sūtra 12/11-13] [T3: IJMHS 2022 "Mānasika hetu cause āma-doṣa by prabhāva"].

### 2.4 Kāla and Deśa (season and land)

- **Season.** Pitta accumulates (*sañcaya*) in Varṣā (rains) and is provoked (*prakopa*) in Śarad (autumn); Vāta is provoked in Varṣā; Kapha in Vasanta [T1: Caraka Sūtra 6, Tasyāśitīya; AH Sūtra 3] [verified x2]. Mādhava's *sva-hetūpacita* is read as this Varṣā accumulation, so Śarad is the classic season of onset/flare [T3: IJMHS 2022; IRJAY 2023] [verified x2]. In Śarad the body "habituated to cold and rain" is suddenly exposed to strong sun, and sour, pungent, hot foods and day-sleep are to be avoided [T1: Caraka Sūtra 6]. Reviews add *Prāvṛṭ* (early rains) as kālaja hetu [T3: IJMHS 2022].
- **Land.** *Jāṅgalaṃ vāta-bhūyiṣṭham, anūpaṃ tu kapholbaṇam, sādhāraṇaṃ samamalam* — dry land is Vāta-predominant, marshy land Kapha-predominant, mixed land balanced [T1: AH Sūtra 1/23]. Kāśyapa: Amlapitta is commoner in **Anūpa deśa** (marshy, humid, water-logged land) and, when all treatment fails, one should **leave that deśa** (*deśāntara-gamana*) [T1 via T3: Kāśyapa Khila 16/44-45; IJAM 2016; IJAPR 2017] [verified x2]. Caraka counts Anūpa among *ahita* (unwholesome) lands [T3: IJAM 2016 ref. 21, Caraka] [single-source].
- **Practical reading:** a humid coastal or monsoon climate, late monsoon-autumn, and the day-sleep/curd/fermented-food culture of such regions stack Kapha on an already Pitta-accumulated body.

### 2.5 Prakṛti (constitution) tendencies

No classical verse names a prakṛti for Amlapitta. The tradition's reasoning: the disease arises in the Āmāśaya "where Kapha and Pitta have their *āśraya*" — *vyādhir āmāśayottho 'yaṃ kapha-pitte tad-āśraye* (Kāśyapa Khila 16/18; corrected 2026-09-18 from 16/22-23) [T1, read directly]; Anūpa deśa people are Kapha (and Vāta) dominant [T1: AH Sūtra 1/23]; the Ūrdhvaga picture is Pitta-Kapha (MN 51/11-12). Hence **Pitta-Kapha and Kapha-Pitta prakṛti** are expected to run most readily to the throat-form, while Vāta-Pitta prakṛti runs to the dry, hoarse, painful Sānila form [T4: inference from the above; discuss with your vaidya] [inference, not a quoted verse]. Case reports show both Pitta-Vāta and Vāta-Kapha prakṛti presenting with Ūrdhvaga signs [T3: JAIMS 2024 case; florajournal 2025 case].

### 2.6 Which nidānas an Ayurvedic clinic actually finds (T3)

A Jamnagar out-patient study of Ūrdhvaga Amlapitta recorded, in descending order of how often the vaidyas found them: **kaṭu rasa** and **amla rasa** (in almost every patient), then *guru*, *snigdha*, *viruddha* and *abhiṣyandi* āhāra, then *atyuṣṇa*, *lavaṇa*, *vidāhi* and *piṣṭānna*, with *adhyaśana* least often. Among vihāra, **divāsvapna** and **drinking water with the meal** led, followed by sedentary living and *vega-dhāraṇa*; among mānasika hetus **cintā** was found in about half of them [T3: AYU 2013;34(4):352-355] [single-source for the ordering; every factor in it is a classical nidāna listed above]. Practical reading: kaṭu and amla rasa are the two āhāra nidānas to remove first, and divāsvapna and water-with-meals the two vihāra nidānas — which is exactly the order Kāśyapa himself gives in 16/3-6.

## 3. Pūrvarūpa and Rūpa (premonitory signs and clinical features)

### 3.1 Pūrvarūpa

No text gives a specific pūrvarūpa for Amlapitta [T3: IJMHS 2022; IRJAY 2023] [verified x2]. The tradition's *tarka* (reasoning): the preceding stages are **Agnimāndya → Ajīrṇa (especially Vidagdhājīrṇa) → mild Annavaha/Rasavaha srotoduṣṭi**, which the vaidya reads as the prodrome [T3: IRJAY 2023; JAIMS 2024 ("Vidagdhājīrṇa … is a premonitory symptom of Amlapitta")] [verified x2]. Practically, for the throat-patient: post-meal heaviness, delayed hunger, faint sour taste, a "thorny" or itchy throat after meals (the pūrvarūpa of Kāsa, §6) are the early warnings.

### 3.2 Sāmānya lakṣaṇa — the cardinal verse (MN 51/2)

> *avipāka-klamotkleśa-tiktāmlodgāra-gauravaiḥ |*
> *hṛt-kaṇṭha-dāhārucibhiś cāmlapittaṃ vaded bhiṣak ||*
> "The physician should call Amlapitta that condition marked by indigestion, exhaustion without exertion, nausea, bitter-and-sour belching, heaviness, burning of the heart-region and throat, and loss of taste." [T1: MN 51/2] [verified x2]

Kāśyapa's list (Khila 16/14-17) adds: *vidbheda* (loose stool), *śiraḥ-śūla* (headache), *hṛt-śūla* (pain in the heart-region), *udara-ādhmāna* (bloating), *aṅga-sāda* (body fatigue), *antra-kūjana* (gurgling), *roma-harṣa* (horripilation), *kaṇṭha-urasi vidāha* (burning in throat and chest), *amlotkleśa* (sour nausea/rising) [T3: IJAM 2016 Table 2; AYUSHDHARA 2025] [verified x2].

### 3.4 Ūrdhvaga Amlapitta — the full verses

> **51/4** *vāntaṃ harit-pītaka-nīla-kṛṣṇam ārakta-raktābham atīva cāmlam | māṃsodakābhaṃ tv atipicchilāccham śleṣmānuyātaṃ vividhaṃ rasena ||*
> Vomitus green, yellow, blue, black, reddish or red, very sour, like meat-washings, very slimy or thin, **followed by Kapha**, of various tastes.
> **51/5** *bhukte vidagdhe 'tha vā apy abhukte karoti tiktāmla-vamiṃ kadācit | udgāram evaṃvidham eva kaṇṭha-hṛt-kukṣi-dāhaṃ śiraso rujaṃ ca ||*
> Bitter-sour vomiting when the food is half-digested or even on an empty stomach; belching of the same kind; burning of throat, heart-region and upper abdomen; head pain.
> **51/6** *kara-caraṇa-dāham auṣṇyaṃ mahatīm aruciṃ jvaraṃ ca kaphapittam | janayati kaṇḍū-maṇḍala-piḍakā-śata-nicita-gātra-rogacayam ||*
> Burning palms and soles, heat, great loss of taste, Kapha-Pitta fever, itching, ring-eruptions and crops of boils. [T1: MN 51/4-6] [verified x2]

Reading for the throat-patient: the "vomiting" of the verse is often only *udgāra* — small bitter-sour risings into the throat, sometimes on an empty stomach at night or on waking (*abhukte*), each followed by a wave of Kapha (mucus) that has to be cleared. The tradition treats that Kapha-tail as part of the disease, not as a separate catarrh.

## 4. Samprāpti (pathogenesis)

### 4.1 Root: Agni fails — Caraka Cikitsā 15/42-44

> *abhojanād ajīrṇātibhojanād viṣamāśanāt | asātmya-guru-śītāti-rūkṣa-sandūṣṭa-bhojanāt ||*
> *vireka-vamana-sneha-vibhramād vyādhi-karṣaṇāt | deśa-kālartu-vaiṣamyād vegānāṃ ca vidhāraṇāt ||*
> *duṣyaty agniḥ sa duṣṭo 'nnaṃ na tat pacati laghv api | apacyamānaṃ śuktatvaṃ yāty annaṃ viṣa-rūpatām ||*
> "From fasting, eating during indigestion, over-eating, irregular eating; from unsuited, heavy, cold, very dry or contaminated food; from mis-applied purgation, emesis or oleation; from wasting by disease; from unsuitability of place, time and season; from suppression of urges — Agni is spoiled. Spoiled, it cannot digest even light food; the undigested food turns **sour (śuktatva)** and takes on the nature of a poison." [T1: Caraka Cikitsā 15/42-44] [verified x2 (carakasamhitaonline; Ghosh & Baghel AYU 2017 cite 15/42-43, 47)]

Then (15/47): *saṃsṛjyamānaṃ pittena dāhaṃ tṛṣṇāṃ mukhāmayān | janayaty amlapittaṃ ca pittajāṃś cāparān gadān* — "joined by Pitta, [the anna-viṣa] generates burning, thirst, **diseases of the mouth and throat**, **Amlapitta** and other Pitta-born diseases" [T1: Caraka Cikitsā 15/47, read directly 2026-09-18] [verified x2: carakasamhitaonline; AYUSHDHARA 2025]. [Corrected 2026-09-18: the verse gives *dāha, tṛṣṇā, mukhāmaya*, not "belching and burning" — and *mukhāmaya* is itself a throat-relevant item, Caraka's own link from āma to the upper passage.]

Agni's four states (15/50-51): *viṣama* (Vāta — erratic), *tīkṣṇa* (Pitta — over-burning), *manda* (Kapha — weak, *durbalo vidahaty annam*, "the weak fire scorches food"), *sama* [T1: Caraka Cikitsā 15/50-51; AH Sūtra 1/8]. The word *vidahati* is the hinge: a weak fire does not cook, it **scorches**, and scorched food is *vidagdha* — the same word Mādhava uses for the Pitta of Amlapitta.

### 4.2 Kāśyapa's sequence and the curd-pot simile (Khila 16/7-13)

Nidāna-sevana → Vāta and the other doṣas aggravated → *mandāgni* → food stays in the Āmāśaya and becomes *śukta* (fermented-sour) through improper burning → if the person, from greed, keeps eating on top of it, Pitta is vitiated in its *drava* (liquid) form → this is Amlapitta. Simile (16/10-11): *aviśuṣke yathā kṣīraṃ prakṣiptaṃ dadhi-bhājane | kṣipram evāmlatām eti kūrcī-bhāvaṃ ca gacchati || **rasa-dhātau tathā vyamle** bhuktaṃ bhuktaṃ vidahyate* — "as milk poured into a curd-pot that has not been dried at once turns sour and curdles, so, **the Rasa-dhātu having turned sour**, whatever is eaten is scorched again and again" [T1: Kāśyapa Khila 16/10-11, read directly 2026-09-18] [T3: IJAM 2016; IJMHS 2022] [verified x2]. The verse puts the souring in the **Rasa dhātu**, not merely in a residue in the stomach — which is why Rasa is the dūṣya (§4.5), and why every fresh meal is scorched again until the Rasa is cleared. Kāśyapa then gives the normal-digestion verse (16/12-13): *dhamyamāna udānena samyak pacati pācakaḥ* — Pācaka digests properly when **fanned by Udāna** and driven by Samāna with the in- and out-breath; the same Udāna is what carries the doṣa upward once digestion fails [T1, read directly 2026-09-18].

Mādhava's version (Madhukośa): Pitta *sañcaya* in Varṣā (natural, kālaja) + continued Pitta-provoking food → *ajīrṇa* → increase of *amla* and *drava* guṇa of Pitta → *vidagdha āhāra-rasa* → mandāgni → Amlapitta [T3: IJMHS 2022] [verified x2 with IRJAY 2023 flow-chart: nidāna → Pitta duṣṭi → ↑amla & drava guṇa → mandāgni → āma/annaviṣa → vidagdha-pāka → ↑amlatā in Āmāśaya → Amlapitta].

### 4.3 The three doṣas and their sub-types in the Ūrdhvaga picture

| Doṣa | Sub-type | Normal seat and function (AH Sūtra 12) | What goes wrong in Ūrdhvaga Amlapitta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pitta | **Pācaka** | Between Pakvāśaya and Āmāśaya; *tyakta-dravatva* — "having given up liquidity", it digests and supports the other four Pittas [T1: AH Sūtra 12/10-12] | Loses its fiery, dry quality and gains *drava* and *amla*: the "fire" becomes a sour liquid — the definition of the disease [T1: MN 51/1; T3 verified x2] |
| Pitta | **Sādhaka** | Heart; intellect, courage, self-sense [T1: AH 12/13] | Worry, fear, anger disturb it; Pācaka, which supports it, is disturbed in turn (§2.3) |
| Kapha | **Kledaka** | Āmāśaya; moistens the food-mass [T1: AH 12/15-16] | Increased by abhiṣyandi/guru/dadhi food and day-sleep; its *snigdha* quality plus Pitta's *drava* give the slimy sour vomitus "like meat-washings, followed by Kapha" [T1: MN 51/4] [T3: IRJAY 2023: "symptoms are due to disturbance of *dravatā* and *uṣṇatā* of Pācaka Pitta and *snigdhatā* of Kledaka Kapha"] |
| Kapha | **Bodhaka** | Tongue; taste perception [T1: AH 12/16] | Coated by risen Kapha-Pitta: *aruci*, *mukha-mādhurya*, *lepa* |
| Kapha | **Avalambaka** | Chest (*uras*); supports heart and the other Kaphas [T1: AH 12/15] | The chest–throat region is the chief Kapha seat (*sutarām uraḥ*); risen Pitta here provokes Kapha to *praseka*, *kaṇṭhopalepa* |
| Vāta | **Samāna** | Near the Agni; receives, digests, separates, releases food [T1: AH 12/8; Caraka Cikitsā 28/8] | Deranged by viṣamāśana/vega-dhāraṇa: Agni becomes *viṣama*/manda; food is held, not moved on |
| Vāta | **Udāna** | Seat *uras*; moves in nose, navel and **throat (gala)**; produces speech, effort, energy, strength, complexion, memory [T1: AH 12/5-6; Caraka Cikitsā 28/7] | Provoked Udāna carries the Pitta-Kapha-āma upward from Āmāśaya to Kaṇṭha; its disturbance is why the **voice** suffers — "Udāna vitiation causes diseases above the collar-bone" [T1: Caraka Cikitsā 28] [T3: JAIMS 2024 ghaṭaka "Doṣa Ūrdhva gati"; Ask-level reviews name Udāna as the carrier] |
| Vāta | **Prāṇa** | Head; moves in chest and **throat**; governs spitting, sneezing, **belching (udgāra)**, breathing, swallowing [T1: AH 12/4-5] | Belching, throat-clearing, cough, swallowing-difficulty are Prāṇa's functions gone wrong; Caraka: Prāṇa vitiation → hikkā, śvāsa, kāsa, pratiśyāya [T1: Caraka Cikitsā 28] |

Caraka's principle: Vāta "owing to its subtlety is the impeller of the other two"; Pitta and Kapha are lame (*paṅgu*) without it, and vitiated Vāta drives them "here and there" causing diseases [T1: Caraka Cikitsā 28 (verse on Vāta as prime mover)]. So the *direction* of Amlapitta — up to the throat or down to the bowel — is decided by Vāta: Udāna/Prāṇa provoked (by vega-dhāraṇa, loud talking, worry, dryness) → Ūrdhvaga; Apāna provoked → Adhoga.

### 4.4 Kapha āvaraṇa and the upward course

- **Āvaraṇa (covering):** Kapha and āma, increased by abhiṣyandi food, obstruct (*saṅga*) the channels; Pitta, unable to pass downward through the blocked Grahaṇī/Pakvāśaya path, is pushed the other way. Ghosh & Baghel describe the samprāpti as "*pitta-kapha-āvṛta Vāta* manifesting at Āmāśaya and Grahaṇī" [T3: AYU 2017;38(1-2):15-23 (PMC5954255)] [single-source for the exact phrase; the mechanism is verified x2 with IRJAY 2023 "vitiated Pitta and Kapha are the key factors for the whole pathological process in Ūrdhvaga"].
- **Vimārgagamana (wrong-path movement):** the srotoduṣṭi type recorded for Amlapitta is *saṅga* + *vimārgagamana* [T3: IJMHS 2022; Ayurlog 2021; AYUSHDHARA 2025] [verified x2] — Pitta-āma travels the wrong way, up the Annavaha channel into Kaṇṭha.
- **Arrival at a Kapha-sthāna:** Kaṇṭha is a seat of Kapha (*uraḥ-kaṇṭha-śiraḥ…kaphasya*) [T1: AH Sūtra 12/3; Caraka Sūtra 20/8 *uraḥ śiro grīvā parvāṇy āmāśayo medaś ca*]. Hot-sour Pitta on a Kapha seat does two things at once: it burns (dāha, galapāka, svarabheda with burning) and it provokes local Kapha to secrete (praseka, kaṇṭhopalepa, śleṣma-niṣṭhīvana). This is the tradition's account of why the throat-patient has both a *raw* throat and a *phlegmy* one.
- **Kāsa loop:** Caraka Cikitsā 18/6 — *adhaḥ pratihato vāyur ūrdhva-srotaḥ-samāśritaḥ | udāna-bhāvam āpannaḥ kaṇṭhe saktas tathorasi* — "Vāyu obstructed below, resorting to the upward channels, taking on the nature of Udāna, lodges in the throat and chest" and produces cough [T1: Caraka Cikitsā 18/6]. The *adhaḥ pratihata* (obstructed below) is precisely the Kapha-āma block of Amlapitta, so a dry or wet cough is a natural extension of the same samprāpti rather than a second disease.

### 4.5 Srotas, dūṣya, adhiṣṭhāna

- **Annavaha srotas** — mūla: Āmāśaya and the left flank (*vāma pārśva*); vitiated by *atimātra, akāla, asātmya bhojana* and *agni-vaiṣamya*; duṣṭi-lakṣaṇa: no desire for food, aruci, avipāka, vomiting [T1: Caraka Vimāna 5/8, 5/18; Sūtra 28] [verified x2].
- **Rasavaha srotas** — mūla: heart and the ten great vessels; vitiated by *guru, śīta, atisnigdha, atimātra* food and **cintā** (worry); duṣṭi-lakṣaṇa include aśraddhā, aruci, āsya-vairasya (bad taste), hṛllāsa, gaurava, tandrā, klama [T1: Caraka Vimāna 5/8, 5/13; Sūtra 28] — several of these are Mādhava's Amlapitta signs, which is why Rasa is the dūṣya.
- **Prāṇavaha srotas** — mūla: heart and the Mahāsrotas (the whole alimentary canal); its involvement explains cough, throat-clearing, breath-catching [T1: Caraka Vimāna 5/8].
- **Udakavaha** (mūla: palate and kloma) — thirst and dry throat when Pitta dries the Kapha [T1: Caraka Vimāna 5/8].
- **Svaravaha / śabdavaha srotas** (Suśruta's channel of voice): doṣas lodging here destroy the voice — Svarabheda [T1: Suśruta Uttara 53/2].
- **Adhiṣṭhāna:** Āmāśaya; **sañcāra-sthāna:** Mahāsrotas; **vyakta-sthāna:** Āmāśaya, and for Ūrdhvaga, Uras and Kaṇṭha [T3: Ayurlog 2021; IJMHS 2022] [verified x2].

## 5. Sādhyāsādhyatā (prognosis) and upadrava (complications)

### 5.1 Mādhava Nidāna 51/7

> *rogo 'yam amlapittākhyo yatnāt saṃsādhyate navaḥ |*
> *cirotthito bhaved yāpyaḥ kṛcchra-sādhyaḥ sa kasyacit ||*
> "This disease named Amlapitta, when new, is cured with effort; long-standing it becomes *yāpya* (only maintainable), and in some persons *kṛcchra-sādhya* (cured with difficulty)." [T1: MN 51/7] [verified x2: easyayurveda; ayureveryday]

Reading: **Nava (recent)** = sādhya but only *yatnāt* — with real effort, meaning strict nidāna-parivarjana (removal of causes) plus treatment; **Jīrṇa / cirotthita (chronic)** = yāpya — controlled while regimen and medicine continue, relapsing when they stop; in some constitutions kṛcchra-sādhya. Reviews add that "if proper dietetic care is taken even the kṛcchra-sādhya type is treatable" [T3: IJMHS 2022]. Discuss with your vaidya which stage yours is; a throat-form that has lasted more than a year with mucus and hoarseness is generally handled as jīrṇa.

### 5.2 Upadravas (complications)

Kāśyapa alone among the early authors lists complications (Khila 16/49): **jvara** (fever), **atisāra** (diarrhoea), **pāṇḍu** (pallor disease — Rasa-Rakta depletion with loss of colour and strength), **śūla** (colic), **śotha** (swelling), **aruci** (anorexia), **bhrama** (giddiness) — and states that one who has these complications together with *dhātu-kṣaya* (wasting of tissues) does not recover [T1 via T3: IJAM 2016; IJMHS 2022] [verified x2]. [Variant resolved 2026-09-18 by direct reading of 16/49 — *jvarātisāra-pāṇḍutva-śūla-śothāruci-bhramaiḥ | upadravair imair juṣṭaḥ kṣīṇa-dhātur na sidhyati*: the verse names **seven** upadravas, and *kṣīṇa-dhātu* is the further condition that makes a patient who has them *asādhya*, not an eighth upadrava. The reviews' "eight" is a miscount.] Yogaratnākara's list adds further items (reviews name *bhrama* and *kaṇḍū* as additions) [T1 via T3: IJNRD 2023; slideshare/JAIMS reviews] [single-source, wording uncertain]. Bhāvaprakāśa's chapter also gives *ariṣṭa* (fatal signs) [T3: IJNRD 2023] [single-source].

Throat-relevant ariṣṭa/asādhya frames the vaidya will watch (from the differential chapters, not from the Amlapitta chapter): Svarabheda that is *sānnipātika* or *kṣayaja*, or in the very weak, aged, emaciated, congenital or obese, is incurable [T1: Suśruta Uttara 53/5-6]; Kāsa that becomes kṣataja/kṣayaja with blood or pus is grave [T1: MN 11]; Rājayakṣmā's eleven signs include hoarseness and cough with wasting [T1: Caraka Cikitsā 8]. Hoarseness with weight loss, blood-streaked sputum, or fever must be taken to your vaidya without delay.

### 5.3 Prognostic modifiers the tradition uses

- *Bala* of patient vs *bala* of disease; *koṣṭha* (a *krūra* — hard — koṣṭha is what the Jamnagar clinic found in the great majority of its Ūrdhvaga patients) [T3: AYU 2013].
- Presence of āma (*sāma* vs *nirāma*): treatment cannot start with unctuous or heavy medicine while āma is present — Kāśyapa forbids any liquid medicine except emetics while the doṣas are excited and moving upward, *doṣotkleśe na sahasā dravam auṣadham ācaret | vamanīyād ṛte tad dhi na samyak paripacyate* — "when the doṣas are excited, no liquid medicine but an emetic should be given at once, for it will not be properly digested" [T1: Kāśyapa Khila 16/22-23, read directly 2026-09-18] [T3: IJAM 2016] [verified x2]. Only after activity and diet have let things settle somewhat (16/23) can a liquid medicine do its *śamana-pācana-bhedana* work.
- Deśa: persisting exposure to Anūpa conditions worsens outlook (Kāśyapa 16/44-45).

## 6. Differential frames the vaidya may weigh

Ayurveda names by *samprāpti* and *lakṣaṇa*, so the same throat can be read under several headings. The vaidya asks which disease's nidāna-samprāpti the patient actually shows.

| Frame | Where described | Core picture | How it is told apart from (or nested inside) Ūrdhvaga Amlapitta |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vidagdhājīrṇa** (Pitta-type indigestion) | MN 6; Suśruta Sūtra 46 | Giddiness, thirst, fainting, sour belching, belching "with a feel of smoke", sweating, internal burning [T1: MN 6] | The **stage before** Amlapitta (JAIMS 2024: "Vidagdhājīrṇa is a premonitory symptom of Amlapitta"); episodic, tied to one meal, no throat-Kapha tail; becomes Amlapitta when it recurs and Pitta itself turns *drava-amla* [T3 verified x2] |
| **Āmājīrṇa** (Kapha-type indigestion) | MN 6 | Heaviness, nausea, swelling of cheeks/eyes, belching "as just after meals" (undigested taste, not sour) [T1] | Belching tastes of food, not sour/bitter; no dāha |
| **Viṣṭabdhājīrṇa** (Vāta-type) | MN 6 | Colic, distension, stool/flatus held, stiffness [T1] | No burning; Vāta signs dominate; overlaps with Sānila Amlapitta when both present |
| **Rasaśeṣājīrṇa** | MN 6 | Aversion to food, unease in heart-region, heaviness [T1] | Morning heaviness without sourness |
| **Pittaja Grahaṇī** | Caraka Cikitsā 15/65-66 | *pūty-amlodgāra-hṛt-kaṇṭha-dāhāruci-tṛḍ-arditaḥ* — foul-sour belching, **throat and heart burning**, aruci, thirst, with bluish-yellow undigested loose stools [T1: Caraka Cikitsā 15/65-66] | Nearly identical upper signs, but Grahaṇī is defined by the **stool** — alternating/undigested, foul, frequent — and by the seat (Grahaṇī, the "holding" organ between Āmāśaya and Pakvāśaya that is the seat of Agni). Charaka's Grahaṇī chapter is also where he places Amlapitta (15/47), so many vaidyas treat throat-Amlapitta with loose stools as Grahaṇī-doṣa first [verified x2] |
| **Pittaja Chardi** (vomiting) | MN 15; 15/7 | *pītaṃ bhṛśoṣṇaṃ haritaṃ satiktaṃ dhūmraṃ ca pittena vamet sadāham* — yellow, very hot, green, bitter, smoky vomitus with burning [T1: MN 15/1, 15/7] | Chardi is a disease of actual, forceful vomiting (five types: three doṣaja, sānnipātika, and *dviṣṭārtha-saṃyoga* — from disgusting sights/smells); Amlapitta's "vomiting" is regurgitation with belching and burning, and Chardi lacks the mandāgni-āma history. | **Kāsa** (cough), five types | Caraka Cikitsā 18; MN 11 | Pūrvarūpa: *śūka-pūrṇa-galāsyatā, kaṇṭhe kaṇḍūḥ, bhojyānām avarodhaḥ* — thorny throat, throat itch, food obstruction [T1: Caraka Cikitsā 18/5]. **Vātaja**: dry, hollow-sounding, *svarabheda-kara*, dry chest and throat, scanty painful sputum. **Pittaja**: yellow sputum, bitter mouth, "smoke from the chest", burning. **Kaphaja**: thick sweet slimy sputum, mouth coated, heaviness, itching. **Kṣataja / Kṣayaja**: blood, pus, wasting [T1: Caraka Cikitsā 18/12-22; MN 11] | The throat-clearing cough of Amlapitta is Kāsa arising *from* the Amlapitta samprāpti (Cikitsā 18/6, §4.4). A vaidya treats it as **Amlapitta-janya Kāsa** when belching/burning precede it; as primary Kāsa when it began with cold, dust, smoke or exertion without digestive signs. Kṣataja/Kṣayaja features (blood, pus, wasting) take it out of the Amlapitta frame entirely |
| **Svarabheda** (hoarseness), six types | Suśruta Uttara 53; Caraka Cikitsā 26 (five types) | Nidāna: loud/prolonged speech, poison, loud study, singing, cold. **Vātaja**: broken, stammering, slow, harsh voice, blackish complexion. **Pittaja**: voice "from deep in the throat", burning on speaking, yellowish. **Kaphaja**: throat obstructed by phlegm, speaks little, worse at night (day-relief). **Sānnipātika**, **Kṣayaja**, **Medoja** (voice lurks in throat, fat-coated) [T1: Suśruta Uttara 53/1-6] [T4: easyayurveda translation] [verified x2 with journal case reports] | Amlapitta-hoarseness comes with the digestive signs and usually reads as Pittaja (burning) or Kaphaja (phlegm, night-worse); primary Svarabheda from voice-abuse or cold lacks belching/burning. | **Hikkā** (hiccup), five types | MN 12; Caraka Cikitsā 17 | Annaja (from hasty eating), Yamalā, Kṣudrā, Gambhīrā, Mahatī; Vāyu rushing up through Prāṇavaha srotas obstructed by Kapha [T1: MN 12] | Shares nidāna (vidāhi, guru, abhiṣyandi, viṣamāśana) and the Kapha-blocks-Prāṇa mechanism; hiccup bouts after meals in an Amlapitta patient are Annajā Hikkā riding on the same samprāpti; Gambhīrā/Mahatī are grave and are a different disease |
| **Tamaka Śvāsa** | MN 12 | Throat gurgling (*ghurghuraka*), throat obstruction, difficulty speaking, relief on expectorating Kapha, prefers sitting [T1: MN 12] | Ūrdhvaga Amlapitta does not give true breathlessness; if wheeze and relief-by-sitting appear, the frame changes and the vaidya must be told |
| **Ādhmāna / Pratyādhmāna, Udāvarta** | Suśruta Uttara 55 (Udāvarta/Ānāha) | Distension with sound; Pratyādhmāna is Kapha-Vāta distension of the Āmāśaya without pain (upper); Udāvarta from suppressed urges [T1: Suśruta Uttara 55] [single-source citation] | Bloating with belching but **no** dāha or sourness = Ādhmāna; with sourness it is Amlapitta with Vāta anubandha. Kāśyapa lists *udara-ādhmāna* among Amlapitta signs, so it can be nested |
| **Rājayakṣmā** (throat signs) | Caraka Cikitsā 8/39-47 | Eleven rūpas: kāsa, aṃsa-abhitāpa (shoulder burning), **svarabheda**, jvara, pārśva-śūla, śiro-ruk, blood-vomiting, expectoration, śvāsa, atisāra, aruci; six-fold and three-fold sets; pūrvarūpa includes pratiśyāya, weakness [T1: Caraka Cikitsā 8] | Hoarseness and cough **with fever, wasting, flank pain and blood** belong here, not to Amlapitta; the vaidya rules it out by weight, strength (*bala*), fever pattern |
| **Kaṇṭha-rogas** (structural throat diseases) | Suśruta Nidāna 16/46-63 | See §7.4 | Amlapitta irritates the throat *from below and within*; the Kaṇṭha-rogas are local swellings/growths. Kaṇṭhaśālūka (a kapha-born thorn-like nodule) and Svaraghna (dry obstructed throat with hoarseness) are the two that most resemble the burning, irritated throat of Ūrdhvaga Amlapitta, and a vaidya may find them coexisting |
| **Galagaṇḍa** | Caraka Sūtra 20/17 (Kapha nānātmaja) | Neck swelling | Only relevant if a visible neck swelling exists; not a differential for symptoms alone |

Anubandha rule the vaidya applies: when two of these coexist, the one whose *nidāna-samprāpti* is primary is the *anubandhya* (principal) and the other the *anubandha* (secondary); treatment targets the principal [T3: IJSR 2024 review of "anubandhānubandhyatva of Ajīrṇa, Amlapitta and Śūla"] [single-source].

## 7. Kaṇṭha and Svara: the throat in Ayurvedic anatomy

### 7.1 Kaṇṭha as a seat of life

> *daśaivāyatanāny āhuḥ prāṇā yeṣu pratiṣṭhitāḥ | śaṅkhau marmatrayaṃ kaṇṭho raktaṃ śukraujasī gudam ||*
> "Ten are the seats in which the life-forces are established: the two temples, the three marmas (head, heart, bladder), **the throat**, blood, semen, ojas and the anus." [T1: Caraka Sūtra 29/3] [verified x2: carakasamhitaonline; IJCRT 2021 Kaṇṭhya review]

Kaṇṭha is built of Māṃsa dhātu, Rasa-Rakta and Kapha, and served by Prāṇavaha and Annavaha srotas; its function "depends on the normal state of Prāṇa and Udāna Vāyu, Māṃsa and Rasa-Rakta dhātu, so vitiation of any of these causes Kaṇṭha or Svara disorders" [T3: Pandhare & Khivsara, IJCRT 2021 9(6)] [single-source summary of standard college teaching].

### 7.2 Kaṇṭha as a Kapha-sthāna

> *uraḥ kaṇṭha-śiraḥ-kloma-parvāṇy āmāśayo rasaḥ | medo ghrāṇaṃ ca jihvā ca kaphasya, sutarām uraḥ ||*
> Chest, **throat**, head, kloma, joints, stomach, rasa, fat, nose and tongue are Kapha's seats, the chest above all. [T1: AH Sūtra 12/3] [verified x2 with Caraka Sūtra 20/8: *uraḥ śiro grīvā parvāṇy āmāśayo medaś ca śleṣma-sthānāni, tatrāpy uro viśeṣeṇa*]

Consequences: Kapha's twenty nānātmaja disorders (Caraka Sūtra 20/17) include *mukhasrāva* (flow from the mouth — *praseka* in Suśruta, Vāgbhaṭa and Mādhava), *kaṇṭhopalepa* (throat coating), *hṛdayopalepa* (the same coating felt at the chest), *śleṣmodgiraṇa* (mucus expectoration), *mukha-mādhurya*, *tandrā*, *gurugātratā*, *apakti* (indigestion), *galagaṇḍa* — the whole "phlegmy throat" cluster [T1, read directly 2026-09-18; corrected — Caraka's own word is *mukhasrāva*]. Pitta's forty (Sūtra 20/14) include *galapāka* (throat inflammation), *tiktāsyatā*, *dhūmaka* (smoky feeling), *amlaka* (sourness), *vidāha*, *antardāha*, *pūtimukhatā* (foul breath) [T1]. Ūrdhvaga Amlapitta at the throat is literally the meeting of these two lists.

### 7.3 Prāṇa and Udāna at the throat; Svaravaha srotas

- Prāṇa: seated in the head, moves through chest and **throat**; governs *ṣṭhīvana* (spitting), *kṣavathu* (sneezing), **udgāra (belching)**, *niḥśvāsa* (breathing), *anna-praveśa* (swallowing) [T1: AH Sūtra 12/4-5].
- Udāna: seated in the chest, moves through nose, navel and **gala (throat)**; governs *vāk-pravṛtti* (speech), *prayatna* (effort), *ūrjā, bala, varṇa, smṛti* [T1: AH Sūtra 12/5-6; Caraka Cikitsā 28/7 "Udāna (navel, thorax, throat): vocalisation, effort, strength, complexion"] [verified x2].
- Suśruta's *svaravaha srotas* (channel of voice) is where doṣas "get lodged and destroy the voice" in Svarabheda [T1: Suśruta Uttara 53/2].
- Kaṇṭhya Mahākaṣāya: Caraka's ten "throat-benefiting" drugs (Sūtra 4) exist because the throat is a distinct therapeutic target; commentators define *kaṇṭhya* as "beneficial for Kaṇṭha and Svara, purifying the organ of speech" [T3: IJCRT 2021] [single-source].

### 7.4 Kaṇṭha marmas

Suśruta Śārīra 6: **Nīlā and Manyā** — four vessels (two Nīlā, two Manyā), one of each on either side of the *kaṇṭha-nāḍī* (windpipe); *sirā-marma*, *vaikalyakara* (deforming); injury "produces dumbness, change of voice (hoarseness) and loss of the faculty of taste" [T1: Suśruta Śārīra 6/66-67, wisdomlib translation] [verified x2 with marma reviews]. **Sirā-mātṛkā** — eight vessels, four on each side of the neck; *sadyaḥ-prāṇahara* (fatal within a day) [T1: Suśruta Śārīra 6/68-69]. A margin of four fingers around these "ten marmas in the neck" is to be respected [T1: Suśruta Śārīra 6/81]. Relevance: the region that Amlapitta inflames is a *marma* region — hoarseness and loss of taste are the very deficits the texts attach to injury here — which is why the tradition treats chronic throat irritation seriously rather than as trivial.

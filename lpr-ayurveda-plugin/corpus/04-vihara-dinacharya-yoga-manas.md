# 04 — Vihara, Dinacharya, Yoga and Manas for Urdhvaga Amlapitta with Kantha Lakshana

Corpus file 04 of the LPR Ayurveda companion. Scope: lifestyle (vihara), daily routine (dinacharya), Yoga (asana, pranayama, shatkarma, mudra, dhyana) and mind (manas) as the tradition applies them to Amlapitta, especially Urdhvaga Amlapitta whose vitiated pitta-kapha rises to the kantha (throat) and produces kantha daha (burning throat), kantha kandu/ghurghurata (throat itch, rattling), praseka (excess salivation / mucus), kasa (cough), swarabheda (hoarseness) and gala-graha-like globus.

Content rule (patient-set): Ayurveda and its sister science Yoga only, treated as a working science on its own terms. No Western physiology, no Western trial commentary. Where a secondary source mixed Western framing, only the Ayurvedic/Yogic reasoning was extracted. The only escalation phrase used is "discuss with your vaidya".

## 0. Framing: why vihara matters in Urdhvaga Amlapitta

| Point | Ayurvedic reasoning | Source / tag |
|---|---|---|
| Amlapitta is agni-mula | Charaka: "all diseases arise from mandagni"; agni is the root of ayus, varna, bala, swasthya; when agni is impaired, food becomes ama → ferments to shukta (sour) → amavisha, and "when this ama meets pitta" the sour, corrosive state called Amlapitta appears. | T1 Charaka Chikitsa 15/3-4, 15/42-45; T3 Ruhi et al. IJAPR 2017 [verified x2] |
| Lifestyle is a named nidana | Charaka Chi 15/42-44 lists among causes of agni-dushti: vishamashana (irregular eating), vega-vidharana (suppressing urges), desha-kala-ritu vaishamya (unsuitable place, time, season), and vyadhi-karshana. Kashyapa Khila 16/3-6 (read in the Sanskrit of the Hemaraja Sharma / Vidyotini edition 2026-09-18, no longer through a secondary review) gives the nidana directly, and three of its items are vihara: "veganam dharanasya ca" (16/4 — suppression of urges), "bhuktva bhuktva divasvapnat" (16/6 — sleeping in the day right after eating, twice over), and "ati-snanavagahanat" (16/6 — excess bathing and prolonged immersion); 16/6 also names antarodaka-pana (drinking water in the middle of a meal) and paryushita-ashana (stale food). Madhava Nidana 51 restates these and adds jagarana. | T1 Charaka Chi 15; T1 Kashyapa Khila 16/3-6 read in the primary text [verified x2 — primary reading now replaces the secondary citation] |
| Kantha is a kapha sthana and udana's road | Vagbhata locates kapha in uras (chest), kantha (throat), shiras, kloma, sandhi, amashaya, rasa, medas, ghrana, jihva; udana vata "dwells in the chest and moves in the throat" (urah-sthah kantha-charah), governing speech (vak-pravritti), effort, strength, complexion and memory. Hence anything that pushes pitta-kapha upward (urdhva-gati) lands on udana's seat and shows as voice, throat and cough symptoms. | T1 AH Sutra 12/3 (kapha sthana), 12/5 (udana) [verified x2 with Sushruta Nidana 1/13 on udana] |
| Urdhvaga = ama-pitta with kapha rising | Madhava 51: Urdhvaga Amlapitta shows vomitus green/yellow/blue/black/red, ati-amla, ati-pichchhila (very sticky), with kantha-hrid-kukshi daha (burning throat/heart/belly), praseka, mukha-madhurya, katu-amla udgara. The "pichchhila" (slimy) and praseka point to kapha riding on pitta — the throat-clearing, mucus-in-throat picture. | T1 Madhava Nidana 51/8-10 via T3 IJNRD 2023, T4 easyayurveda Amlapitta [verified x2] |
| Therefore | Vihara aims at four things: (1) protect agni (kala-bhojana, no ajirna-bhojana, no vega dharana); (2) keep pitta cool and kapha light (sheeta-vihara, no divaswapna, moderate vyayama); (3) keep udana/prana moving downward-and-smooth (no urdhva-pushing postures, voice rest, gandusha, nasya); (4) keep manas sattvika (no krodha-chinta-shoka-bhaya at meals). | Synthesis of rows above |

## 1. Classical Apathya and Pathya Vihara for Amlapitta

### 1.1 Apathya vihara (what the texts forbid)

| Apathya | Text and mechanism | Relevance to kantha symptoms | Source / tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Divaswapna (day sleep), esp. bhuktva-divaswapna (sleeping after eating) | Charaka: day sleep outside Grishma vitiates kapha and pitta; effects include agni-nasha, hrillasa (nausea), pinasa, kasa, gala-roga (throat disorders), staimitya, gaurava. Kashyapa/Madhava name it a root cause of Amlapitta. | Directly produces throat disorders, cough and kapha praseka — the kapha layer of LPR-type symptoms. | T1 Charaka Su 21/44-49 (verse 47 verified verbatim 2026-09-18: "...kandus tandra kaso galamayah"); T1 Kashyapa Khila 16/6 read in the primary Sanskrit ("bhuktva bhuktva divasvapnat") and T1 Madhava 51; T3 Yogaratnakara list via IJAPR 2017 [verified x2] |
| Ratri jagarana (night vigil) | Charaka Su 21/50: night wakefulness makes the body ruksha (dry) — vata prakopa; vata then carries pitta upward (urdhva-gati). Charaka Su 5 lists ratri-jagarana among states in which dhumapana is forbidden. | Dry throat, hoarse morning voice, vata-pitta type swarabheda. | T1 Charaka Su 21/50; T3 IJNRD 2023 (Kashyapa/Madhava "jagarana") [verified x2] |
| Vega-vidharana / vega-dharana (suppressing urges) | Charaka Su 7/3-4 and AH Su 4/1 together forbid holding: udgara (belch), chhardi (vomit), kshavathu (sneeze), kasa (cough), mutra, purisha, adhovata, jrimbha, ashru, kshudha, trishna, nidra, shrama-shvasa, retas. Each text names thirteen and the thirteens differ: Charaka's includes udgara and omits kasa; AH's includes kasa and omits udgara (though AH still treats udgara-nigraha separately at Su 4/5). The merged list above is what applies here. Yogaratnakara specifically names chhardi-vega-dharana as Amlapitta apathya. See §3. | Holding belch/cough/vomit drives vata upward and jams kapha in the kantha. | T1 Charaka Su 7/3-4; T1 AH Su 4/1; T1 Yogaratnakara via T4 easyayurveda [verified x2] |
| Ati-vyayama (excess exercise) and vyayama soon after food | Charaka Su 7/31-35 and AH Su 2/10-13: exercise beyond ardha-shakti causes shrama, klama, kshaya, trishna, raktapitta, pratamaka, kasa, jvara, chhardi. Sushruta Chi 24: vyayama is contraindicated after meals (bhuktavan). | Kasa and chhardi appear in the list of over-exercise effects; exertion after meals pushes the meal upward. | T1 Charaka Su 7/33 (the over-exercise effects verse), 7/34-35 (the six excesses); T1 AH Su 2/12-13; T1 Sushruta Chi 24/38-51 [verified x2; verse roles corrected 2026-09-18] |
| Ati-maithuna | Charaka Su 7/29 lists ati-vyavaya among the kayika urges to restrain; Su 7/34 names vyavaya alongside vyayama, hasya (laughing), bhashana (speaking), adhva-gamana (walking) and ratri-jagarana as the six that must not be done to excess even by those habituated to them, "lest one perish like a lion dragging an elephant" (7/35). AH Su 2/12 separately excludes the sexually depleted from exercise. Ojas and udana are depleted; vata rises. | Weak udana = weak voice; dryness of throat. | T1 Charaka Su 7/29, 7/34-35 (verse content verified verbatim 2026-09-18); T1 AH Su 2/12 [corrected: 7/34 is the six-excesses verse, not an exercise-exclusion list] |
| Atapa sevana (sun exposure) and agni-sevana (sitting by fire/heat) | Pitta-prakopa hetu in all texts; Sharad regimen forbids atapa; Yogaratnakara lists atapa-sevana as Amlapitta apathya. | Heat liquefies pitta (vilayana) and makes it flow upward; throat burning worsens. | T1 AH Su 3 (Sharad), Charaka Su 6; T3 IJAPR 2017 (Yogaratnakara table) [verified x2] |
| Ushna-jala shiro-snana (hot water on the head) | AH Su 2/17 and Sushruta Chi 24/59: hot water poured on the head harms eyes and hair (chakshu-bala-hara); bath only with warm-not-hot water below the neck. Head is a kapha-pitta seat; heating it aggravates urdhva-jatru pitta. | Avoid steaming the head/throat with very hot showers; use lukewarm. | T1 AH Su 2/17; T1 Sushruta Chi 24/59 [verified x2] |
| Ati-snana and avagahana (excess bathing, prolonged immersion) | Kashyapa Khila 16/6, verified in the Sanskrit 2026-09-18: "ati-snanavagahanat" stands in the nidana list itself. Madhava 51 repeats it. Snana immediately after food is separately forbidden (Sushruta Su 46; AH Su 8). | Cold-immersion after meals chills agni; long hot baths heat pitta. | T1 Kashyapa Khila 16/6 read in the primary text; T1 Madhava 51 [verified x2 — upgraded from [single-source] 2026-09-18] |
| Ati-bhashana, uccha-bhashana (excess or loud speaking) | Charaka Su 7/28 (vachika dharaniya vega: atimatra-bhashana), Su 8/18 ("one should not speak too much"); Sushruta Uttara 53: hoarseness arises from ati-uchcha-bhashana, loud reading, cold exposure, injury. Charaka Su 7/34 names bhashana (speaking) among the six things not to be done in excess even by the habituated; AH Su 2/12 excludes the uchchair-bhashi (one who must speak loudly) from exercise. HYP 1/15: "prajalpa" (chatter) is one of six things that destroy yoga. | The most direct voice-protective rule in the classics. See §4. | T1 Charaka Su 7/28, 7/34, 8/18; T1 Sushruta Uttara 53/2 (causes verse — corrected from 53/3 against the Bhishagratna text 2026-09-18); T1 AH Su 2/12; T1 HYP 1/15 [verified x2] |
| Krodha, chinta, shoka, bhaya (anger, worry, grief, fear) | Two consecutive verses, both verified verbatim 2026-09-18. Charaka Vimana 2/8: food and drink taken by one whose mind is scorched by kama, krodha, lobha, moha, irshya, hri, shoka, mana, udvega or bhaya "corrupts even that into ama" (kama-krodha-lobha-moha-irshya-hri-shoka-mana-udvega-bhaya-upatapta-manasa ... tad apy amam eva pradushayati). Charaka Vimana 2/9: even wholesome food taken in the right quantity is not digested by one afflicted by chinta (anxious thought), shoka (grief), bhaya (fear), krodha (anger), duhkha-shayya (an uncomfortable bed) or prajagara (night vigil) — "cinta-shoka-bhaya-krodha-duhkha-shayya-prajagaraih". Yogaratnakara lists chinta-krodha-shoka as Amlapitta apathya. Pitta-prakopa hetu (AH Su 12; Charaka Chi 15). | Sadhaka pitta and pachaka pitta share one dosha; agitated mind = agitated agni = vidagdha pitta. Note that Vi 2/9 puts a bad bed and a short night in the same list as anger — sleep and mind are one nidana here. See §6. | T1 Charaka Vi 2/8 (the kama-krodha-lobha-moha list → ama) and T1 Charaka Vi 2/9 (the chinta-shoka-bhaya-krodha-duhkhashayya-prajagara list → food not digested), both Sanskrit-verified 2026-09-18. The earlier single citation "Vi 2/9: irshya, bhaya, krodha, lobha, shoka, dainya, dvesha" conflated the two verses and mis-rendered both; T3 IJAPR 2017 (Yogaratnakara) [verified x2] |
| Sleeping immediately after meals; lying supine after meals | Charaka Su 8/22 (sadvritta): do not sleep immediately after eating; Sushruta Su 46: do not sleep, bathe, run, ride after food; IJNRD (Kashyapa-based review): avoid supine lying after eating, left-lateral is the advised position if one must lie. | Prime cause of urdhva-gati of the meal into the kantha. | T1 Charaka Su 8/22; T1 Sushruta Su 46/490-491 [verified x2] |
| Tambula (betel), madya (alcohol), dhumrapana (tobacco), tea/coffee | AH Su 2/7: tambula forbidden to those with pitta-rakta disorders, kshina, ruksha, in chhardi and murchha; madya is a pitta-prakopa nidana of Amlapitta in Madhava; tobacco/beverages are agantuja hetu in reviews. | All are ushna-tikshna and vidahi on the throat. See §8. | T1 AH Su 2/7; T3 IJAPR 2017; T4 easyayurveda gastritis [verified x2] |

## 2. Dinacharya tailored for Urdhvaga Amlapitta with throat symptoms

### 2.1 The day at a glance

| Ayurvedic time | Practice | Amlapitta-kantha tailoring | Source / tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brahma muhurta (last 3 ghatis of night, ~1.5 h before sunrise; vata kala) | Wake; check whether last night's food is digested (jirna-ahara lakshana: light body, clear belch, appetite, natural urges) | If the belch is still sour/food-smelling, treat the morning as ajirna: skip ushah-pana volume, take only sips of warm water, no abhyanga, no nasya until cleared. | T1 AH Su 2/1 ("brahme muhurte uttishthet ... jirna-ajirnam nirupayan") [verified x2 with Charaka Su 5] |
| Immediately on waking | Ushah-pana (see 2.2) | Warm or room-temperature, small volume for pitta-kapha in the kantha. | T1 Bhavaprakasha Purvakhanda 5; T4 easyayurveda Ushapana [verified x2] |
| After ushah-pana | Mala-visarjana (evacuate bowel and bladder) without straining; never postpone | Purisha-vega-dharana turns apana upward (udavarta). Charaka Su 7/8 gives pakvashaya-shula (colic), shirah-shula, retention of stool and flatus, pindikodveshtana (calf cramps) and adhmana, with treatment at 7/9. Pratishyaya and hrid-uparodha come from AH Su 4, not from Charaka. | T1 Charaka Su 7/8-9 (verified verbatim 2026-09-18); T1 AH Su 4/3 [verified x2; attribution corrected] |
| Then | Danta-dhavana, jihva-nirlekhana (2.3) | Tikta-kashaya twigs; read the tongue coating for ama. | T1 AH Su 2/2-3; Sushruta Chi 24/4-13; Charaka Su 5/71-75 [verified x2] |
| Then | Gandusha / kavala (2.4) | Tila taila or Yashtimadhu-ksheera for kantha. | T1 AH Su 22/1-12; Charaka Su 5/78-80 (the taila-gandusha verse 80 verified verbatim 2026-09-18) [verified x2] |
| Then (kapha kala of morning) | Pratimarsha nasya (2.5) | Two drops Anu taila; only when nasal passages clear and no ajirna. | T1 AH Su 20/28-29 (corrected 2026-09-18); Charaka Su 5/57-63 [verified x2] |
| Then | Abhyanga (2.6) — head, ears, feet at minimum | Pitta-shamaka oils; not on days of ajirna or heavy kapha. | T1 AH Su 2/8-9; Charaka Su 5/81-93 [verified x2] |
| Then | Vyayama at ardha-shakti (2.7); then Yoga asana/pranayama (§5) | Stop at the first sign of sweating and quickened breath. | T1 Charaka Su 7/31-33; AH Su 2/10-11 [verified x2] |
| Then | Snana (2.8) — warm below neck, cool/lukewarm on head | No very hot showers on the head. | T1 AH Su 2/16-17; Sushruta Chi 24/59 [verified x2] |
| Sunrise to ~10:00 (kapha kala) | Light breakfast only if hungry; otherwise wait | Kapha kala: heavy breakfast increases praseka. | T1 AH Su 8 (kala-bhojana) [single-source in this framing] |
| Midday (pitta kala, agni strongest) | Pradhana bhojana (main meal) following Charaka Vi 1/24-25 rules | Main meal at noon when agni is strongest is the Amlapitta patient's chief safeguard. | T1 Charaka Vi 1/24-25; Charaka Vi 2/3 (matra) [verified x2] |
| After the main meal | Sit a while "like a king"; walk 100 steps (shatapavali); lie briefly on the left side (vamakukshi) without sleeping (2.9) | Never lie supine; never sleep in the day. | T1 Sushruta Su 46/490-491; T1 Bhavaprakasha (bhojana vidhi) [verified x2, verse numbering varies] |
| Afternoon (vata kala from ~14:00) | Work; sip warm water; voice rest breaks | See §4. | T1 Charaka Su 8/18 [single-source] |
| Sayam (evening, before sunset) | Light supper (laghu, sheeta-virya, snigdha), ideally before sunset and at least 3 hours before sleep | AH Su 8: the evening meal must be lighter; Sushruta: do not sleep on a full stomach. | T1 AH Su 8; Sushruta Su 46 [verified x2] |
| After supper | Shatapavali again; then quiet, cool activities; chandra-kirana; Bhramari/Om; no screens in the sense of no indriya-atiyoga | Sharad-style evening. | T1 AH Su 3 (Sharad) applied; T1 Charaka Su 21/52-54 [verified x2] |
| Nidra (by ~22:00, kapha kala of night) | Sleep at habitual time on a comfortable bed; head not to the north; see 2.10 for side and head elevation | Sleeping in the kapha kala of night (before ~22:00) makes sleep come easily and keeps pitta from rising at midnight. | T1 Charaka Su 21/52-54; T1 AH Su 2 sadvritta [verified x2] |

### 2.2 Ushah-pana (morning water)

| Question | Tradition's answer | Source / tag |
|---|---|---|
| Classical source | Not in Charaka, Sushruta or AH as a daily-routine item; first codified by Bhavaprakasha (Purvakhanda, Dinacharyadi prakarana) and Bhoja: drink 8 prasriti of water at the end of night (ushah-kala) before sunrise; stated to remove arsha, shotha, grahani, jvara, udara, medo-roga, disorders of vata-pitta-kapha, and to confer long life. | T1 Bhavaprakasha PK 5 via T4 easyayurveda Ushapana 2021 [verified x2 with T3 IJAM 2024 "Ushah Pana Rasayanam"] |
| Quantity | 8 prasriti (~640 ml) is the textual full dose; Hebbar notes this is a maximum for the strong; beginners start with 1–2 glasses. For Amlapitta with kapha praseka, the practical vaidya advice is 1 glass (about 250 ml). | T4 easyayurveda [single-source on tapering] |
| Cold vs warm — the debate | Bhoja: "paryushita jala" (water kept overnight, i.e. cool). Hebbar and most modern vaidyas: warm water for kapha-ama states because sheeta jala on a kapha-laden amashaya at kapha-kala increases praseka and mandagni; cool water only for pure pitta with strong agni. | [variant] T4 easyayurveda; T3 IJAM 2024 |
| Amlapitta-kantha decision | Warm (ushna, not hot) water, sipped, 150–250 ml; increase only if the tongue is clean and belch is clear. If the throat is burning (pitta-dominant) use room-temperature; if throat is full of mucus (kapha-dominant) use warm with a slice of ginger only if no daha. | Synthesis; discuss with your vaidya |
| Copper vessel water | Tamra-jala is kapha-medo-hara per Bhavaprakasha/Rasa texts; acceptable for kapha-dominant Amlapitta; avoid if pitta-daha dominates (tamra is ushna). | T4 easyayurveda [single-source] |

### 2.3 Danta-dhavana and Jihva-nirlekhana

| Item | Rule | Source / tag |
|---|---|---|
| Twig | 12 angula long, little-finger thick, from a tree of kashaya (astringent), katu (pungent) or tikta (bitter) taste: arka, nyagrodha, khadira, karanja, kakubha (AH); nimba (tikta), khadira (kashaya), madhuka (madhura), karanja (katu) (Sushruta). | T1 AH Su 2/2; T1 Sushruta Chi 24/4-12; Charaka Su 5/71-72 [verified x2] |
| For Amlapitta-kantha | Prefer nimba (tikta, pitta-kapha-hara) or khadira (kashaya, kapha-hara, kantha-shodhana). Avoid lavana- or katu-heavy tooth powders (they are ushna and provoke pitta in the mouth-throat). Powder alternative: Triphala + a little nimba. | T1 AH Su 2/2 tastes; T4 practice [single-source on powder] |
| Who must not brush with a twig | Ajirna (indigestion), chhardi, shvasa, kasa, jvara, ardita, trishna, mukha-paka, hrid-roga, netra-shiro-karna roga. Sushruta adds kantha-roga and mukha-vrana. Note: "kasa" and "kantha-roga" appear — on days of acute throat pain or violent cough, use gentle finger-and-powder cleaning only. | T1 AH Su 2/3; T1 Sushruta Chi 24/12 [verified x2] |
| Jihva-nirlekhana | Scraper of gold, silver, copper, or a tree twig; 10 angula; curved, not sharp; removes jihva-mala, restores taste, and "the mala scraped from the tongue is a sign of ama" (Sushruta). | T1 Sushruta Chi 24/13; T1 Charaka Su 5/75; T1 AH Su 2/? (jihva-nirlekhana) [verified x2] |
| Reading the tongue | Thick white/pale coating = kapha-ama (expect praseka, throat mucus, heaviness: keep breakfast light, warm water). Yellow, thin, with red edges and burning = pitta-ama (expect kantha daha, sour belch: sheeta-madhura foods). Dry, cracked, dark = vata (dry throat, hoarse voice: snigdha foods, more abhyanga). Clean pink tongue = nirama; full routine may proceed. | T1 Charaka Vi 2 (ama lakshana) + T4 clinical convention [single-source on reading] |

### 2.4 Gandusha and Kavala for the kantha

| Point | Text | Source / tag |
|---|---|---|

| Hours | Sleep in the first kapha kala of night (by ~22:00); rise in brahma muhurta; the aged and the pitta-vata person may take 6–7 hours, the kapha person less. | T1 AH Su 2/1; Charaka Su 21 [verified x2] |

### 5.2 Asanas — recommended, with timing relative to meals

**Two standing rules govern every row below, and no row overrides them.** (1) *Meal timing* (§5.1): asana 3–4 hours after a full meal and 1–2 hours after a light one. (2) *Urdhva-gati*: in Urdhvaga Amlapitta the disease itself is the upward movement of vidagdha pitta and kapha into the kantha, so any posture that compresses the amashaya, puts the head below the heart, or lifts the pelvis above the chest is moving things in the diseased direction. Such postures belong to a calm phase, on an empty stomach, and are omitted entirely in a flare (§8A). Discuss the whole sequence with your vaidya and learn it from a qualified teacher.

### 5.4 Pranayama

**Two standing rules govern every row below.** (1) *Meal timing*: pranayama is done on an empty stomach — 3–4 hours after a full meal, 1–2 hours after a light one. The single exception is soft Bhramari and Om chanting, which use no abdominal effort and no retention and may be done sitting upright after supper. (2) *Urdhva-gati*: every forceful or heating breath (Kapalabhati, Bhastrika, Surya Bhedana, Agnisara) and every retention or lock (kumbhaka, jalandhara, uddiyana, mula bandha) drives prana and pitta upward into udana's seat — omit them while the kantha is symptomatic. HYP 2/15-17 is the classical warning in the tradition's own voice: faulty pranayama itself produces hikka, shvasa, kasa and pain of head, ears and eyes.

### The head-elevation resolution (the item this pass was asked to settle)
A headless test of the plugin produced the advice "lie on the left side with the head raised". That sentence fuses one classical instruction with one that does not exist. §2.10 has been rewritten so the corpus cannot generate it again. The left side (vama-parshva) is genuinely classical — Sushruta Sutra 46, verified verbatim — but it is the **post-meal**, brief, **awake** rest, and lying down to sleep after eating is the opposite of what the texts say (Charaka Su 8/22, Charaka Su 21/44-49, Kashyapa Khila 16/6 "bhuktva bhuktva divasvapnat", Sushruta Su 46 "the after-meal siesta should not be long and continuous"). Head-of-bed elevation has **no classical basis whatever**: it is absent from Charaka Sutra 21, Charaka Sutra 8, AH Sutra 2, Sushruta Sutra 46 and Sushruta Chikitsa 24, and the practitioner source re-read for this pass (easyayurveda's Amlapitta page) gives no sleeping-position or head-elevation advice at all. It is recorded as a practitioner-level convention taken from outside the tradition, carries no tier tag, and must not be presented as Ayurvedic. In its place §2.10 now states the four measures the tradition actually gives against night-time urdhva-gati: light supper before sunset, a three-hour gap, no supine lying and no day sleep after food, and shatapavali with Vajrasana. A fourth row spells out explicitly why "left side" and "head raised" must never be spoken in one sentence.

**No classical source exists for two items and they must never be asserted:** tight abdominal clothing, and head-of-bed elevation. Both are marked in place (§8 and §2.10) and the head-elevation question is treated at length in 2.10 because a downstream test of the plugin produced the fused advice "lie on the left side with the head raised" — the left side is classical **for the post-meal rest only**, the head-raising is not classical at all, and the two must not be given as one sentence.

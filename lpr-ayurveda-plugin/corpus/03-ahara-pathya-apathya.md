## 0. The disease frame this diet serves (one-paragraph orientation)

- Amlapitta is "pitta that has acquired the amla (sour) guna" (Chakrapani, cited in T3 IJAM 2016). Kashyapa was the first to treat it as a separate disease (KS.Khila 16); Madhava Nidana 51 split it into Urdhvaga (upward-moving) and Adhoga (downward) [verified x2: T3 IJAM 2016; T3 IRJAY 2023].
- Urdhvaga Amlapitta lakshana (MN 51/5-7): vomiting of green/yellow/black/sour/slimy matter, amla-tikta udgara (sour-bitter belching), hrit-kantha-kukshi daha (burning in chest, throat, upper abdomen), shirah-shula, kandu/mandala/pidaka, and the general set avipaka, klama, utklesha, gaurava, aruchi [verified x2: T4 easyayurveda MN 51; T3 IRJAY 2023].
- Samprapti in Kashyapa, verse numbers now read in the primary (Kashyapa Samhita, Khilasthana adhyaya 16 "Amlapitta-chikitsita", Hemaraja Sharma ed., 2026-09-18): 16/1-2 definition — one who over-uses pitta-prakopaka anna-pana does not digest his pitta properly; it becomes VIDAGDHA and attains AMLA-BHAVA, and that is Amlapitta; 16/3-6 nidana; 16/7 mandagni; 16/13-15 samanya lakshana (vidbheda, koshtha-gaurava, amla-utklesha, shirah-shula, hrit-shula, adhmana, angasada, antra-kujana, ROMAHARSHA and burning in KANTHA and URA — the throat is named by Kashyapa himself); 16/16-17 the three upashaya types; 16/18 "vyadhir amashayottho'yam kapha-pitte tadashraye" — the disease arises from the amashaya, with kapha and pitta seated there. Simile (via T3): milk poured into a curd-pot sours at once. Simile: milk poured into a curd-pot sours at once. Charaka states the same chain for ajirna (Ch.Chi 15/42-49): apakva anna → ama → shukta → amavisha; symptoms amla udgara, hrid-daha, kantha-daha, aruchi [verified x2: T1 carakasamhitaonline; T3 IJAM 2016].
- Dosha: Pitta (pachaka) + Kapha (kledaka) are primary in Urdhvaga; Samana and Udana vayu carry the vitiated pitta-kapha upward to hrit, kantha, shiras (T3 IRJAY 2023; T3 IJMHS 2022 lists Vata: Samana, Udana) [verified x2].
- Therapeutic consequence for diet: reduce amla, drava, ushna, tikshna, vidahi, guru, abhishyandi; favour tikta-madhura-kashaya, laghu, sheeta, mildly snigdha; protect agni; avoid ama; keep Udana calm (no eating late, no lying flat on a full stomach, no talking while eating).
- Kashyapa's unique preventive note, primary verses located 2026-09-18: "anupadeshe prayena sambhavaty esha dehinam || tasmaj jangalajair enam aushadhaih samupakramet | aprashamyati chet asmin api deshantaram vrajet" (KS.Khila 16/44-45) — the disease arises mostly in those living in ANUPA desha; treat with jangala-grown drugs; if it still does not subside, move to another desha. 16/46 adds that the best desha is the one where health and well-wishers are. Charaka's agrya agrees: "anupo'hitadeshanam" — marshland is the foremost of unwholesome lands (Ch.Su 25/40) [verified x2: T1 KS.Khila 16/44-46; T1 Ch.Su 25/40].
- Kashyapa's closing prognosis (KS.Khila 16/42): "yuktahara-viharasya yukta-vyayama-sevinah | shuktako'yam alolasya shamyaty atmavatah satah" — this shuktaka (his name for Amlapitta) subsides in one whose food and conduct are measured, whose exercise is measured, who is ALOLA (free of greed of the tongue) and self-possessed. This verse, not any single dravya, is the charter of this whole file [single-source: T1 KS.Khila 16/42 (primary)].

- Kashyapa's pathya verse, now read in the primary (KS.Khila 16/38-40, 2026-09-18): "puranah shalayo mudga masurah saharenavah | gavyam sarpih payo va'pi jangalash cha mrigadvijah || kalayashakam pautikam vasapushpam savastukam | yani chanyani shakani TIKTANI cha LAGHUNI cha || bhojanenatishasyante yach chanyad AVIDAHI cha | tat-satmyanam prayoganam yathoktanam cha shilanam ||". Note what the primary does NOT contain: no yava, no godhuma, no patola, no karavellaka, no takra — those enter from YR/BR/BP, exactly as the table's columns show. - The bala-agni clause is KS.Khila 16/41: "lashunasya haritakyah pippalyah sarpishas tatha | madyayash cha jirnayah kalagni-bala-vriddhaye" — garlic, haritaki, pippali, ghee and OLD (jirna) wine, for the increase of agni and bala. [variant]: lashuna, pippali and madya are ushna and are avoided by later authors for pitta, and madya is apathya in every other Amlapitta list including Kashyapa's own nidana at 16/4; treat 16/41 as a kapha-dominant, agni-weak clause, not a general licence. Discuss with your vaidya before using garlic.
- Internal tension inside Kashyapa, recorded honestly: 16/4 counts "madyanam GORASASYA cha" (milk products) among the nidana, while 16/38 gives gavya sarpi and payas as pathya. The reconciliation the text itself supplies is ATI-SEVANA — the nidana verse governs excess and wrong form (raw, cold, curd, cheese-like), the pathya verse governs measured boiled milk and a spoon of ghee.
- CCRAS/AYUSH column: the Ministry's public FAQ on Amlapitta (S32) lists wheat, old rice, barley, green gram as pathya and spicy, sour, pungent, fried, oily as apathya (T2; namayush.gov.in; text seen via search summary only, host unreachable on 2026-09-17 and again 2026-09-18) [single-source at T2; matches T1].

## 2. Rasa, Guna, Virya, Vipaka logic for the Amlapitta plate

### 2a. Six rasas and the doshas (Ch.Su 26/42-45; AH.Su 1/14-15) [verified x2]

| Rasa | Increases | Decreases | Over-use effects relevant to the kantha (Ch.Su 26/43) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Madhura | Kapha | Pitta, Vata | Kapha, sthaulya, alasya, galaganda, kantha-gata kapha (praseka) |
| Amla | Pitta, Kapha (kledaka), rakta | Vata | "paridahati kantham uro hridayam cha" — burns throat, chest, heart region; danta-harsha; kapha vilayana (kapha liquefies — more praseka) |
| Lavana | Pitta, Kapha, rakta | Vata | Raktapitta, trishna, daha, vali-palita, kshara-like corrosion; Sushruta names "amlika" among lavana-excess diseases (T3 IJAM) |
| Katu | Pitta, Vata | Kapha | Daha, trishna, bhrama, kantha-shosha (dry throat), murcha, tremor |
| Tikta | Vata | Pitta, Kapha | Over-use dries dhatus; in Amlapitta tikta is the chief shamana rasa (Kashyapa's drugs are tikta; Ch.Chi 15 uses tikta ghrita) |
| Kashaya | Vata | Pitta, Kapha | Over-use: asya-shosha, hrit-pida, vak-graha (speech obstruction), srotorodha — so use kashaya lightly, not as the base |

Rule for Amlapitta diet: base of MADHURA (bulk, nourishment, pitta-vata shamana: rice, wheat, milk, ghee, sugar, ripe sweet fruit) + TIKTA (patola, karavella, karkotaka, bitter greens, ama-pachana without heat) + a little KASHAYA (mudga, dadima, yava, amalaki: grahi, drying the drava of pitta). Amla-Lavana-Katu minimal. This is the AH.Su 13 rule: pitta needs svadu-sheeta; kapha needs tikshna-ruksha — Amlapitta being pitta-kapha, the meeting point is tikta (which is sheeta AND ruksha-laghu) [verified x2: AH.Su 13/4-12 via T4 easyayurveda; T3 IJAM 2016 "tikta rasa = ruksha + sheeta guna against ushna + drava of pitta"].

### 2b. Vipaka (Ch.Su 26/57-58; effects Ch.Su 26/61-62 and AH.Su 9/20-21) [verified x2]

- Madhura and lavana rasa → madhura vipaka (kapha↑, vata-pitta↓, srishta mala-mutra).
- Amla rasa → amla vipaka (pitta↑).
- Katu, tikta, kashaya → katu vipaka (vata↑, kapha↓, baddha mala).
- Consequence: the reliable tests for a food in Amlapitta are (i) vipaka not amla and (ii) virya not ushna. Amla-vipaka foods to minimise: dadhi, kulattha, yava? — NOTE [variant]: AH.Su 6/13 gives yava as "amla vipaka" in one reading while Su.Su 46 gives katu vipaka; Bhavaprakasha gives katu; all agree yava is sheeta and kapha-pitta hara, and every Amlapitta pathya list includes it — the sheeta virya and laghu-ruksha gunas dominate. Keep yava.
- Exceptions by prabhava: amalaki is amla in rasa but madhura in vipaka and sheeta in virya → pitta-hara (AH.Su 6/158); dadima (sweet variety) is madhura-kashaya, pitta-hara. These are the only "sour-tasting" items the classical lists admit.

## 3. Ashta Ahara Vidhi Visheshayatana and Ahara Vidhi Vidhana as eating rules (Ch.Vi 1/21-25) [verified x2: T1 carakasamhitaonline; T4 easyayurveda]

### 3a. The eight determinants (Ch.Vi 1/21-22)

| Ayatana | Meaning (text's own example) | Application to reflux reaching the kantha |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Prakriti | Natural quality of the substance ("masha is guru, mudga is laghu") | Choose laghu-sheeta-avidahi by nature (§1); do not try to "fix" masha or kulattha by cooking |
| 2. Karana | Processing: contact with water and fire, cleansing, churning, place, time, infusing, steeping | Old rice boiled soft; barley as yavagu not roti; churned takra (not curd); ghee heated once; grains washed; avoid deep frying and dry roasting (vidahi) |
| 3. Samyoga | Combination producing properties absent in the parts | Viruddha (§5): milk + sour; ghee + honey equal; curd + fruit; fish + milk; hot drink + honey |
| 4. Rashi | Quantity — sarvagraha (whole meal) and parigraha (each item) | §4: two parts solid, one liquid, one empty; guru items to half-satiety (Ch.Su 5/7) |
| 5. Desha | Land of origin and land of the eater (jangala / anupa / sadharana) | Kashyapa: Amlapitta favoured by anupa desha, and the remedy is jangala-desha dravyas BEFORE any thought of moving (KS.Khila 16/44-45); in cold-dry lands the vata share rises (§8) |
| 6. Kala | Time: season, time of day, stage of disease | Sharad and Varsha need most restraint (§8); evening meal light and early; during an utklesha/ama phase — langhana (§9) |
| 7. Upayoga-samstha | Rules of use (the ten rules below), dependent on agni | Every rule below reduces the upward movement of pitta-kapha into the kantha |
| 8. Upayokta | The eater: satmya (habituation), prakriti, agni, bala | A pitta-kapha person with a kantha-seated disease is the upayokta; satmya to coffee or chilli does not make them pathya, it only softens their harm (Ch.Su 26/104-106: satmya, small quantity, deeptagni, youth protect) |

### 3b. The ten rules of eating (Ch.Vi 1/24-25) and why each matters for the throat

| Rule (Sanskrit) | Text's reason | Kantha/reflux reading |
|---|---|---|
| Ushnam ashniyat — eat warm | tastes well, stimulates agni, digests quickly, normalises vata, reduces kapha | Cold food congeals kledaka kapha in the amashaya and is the direct cause of praseka and kantha-kandu; warm food keeps the samana-udana movement smooth |
| Snigdham — eat unctuous | stimulates agni, digests quickly, normalises vata, firms indriyas, gives bala and varna | Dry meals (ruksha) rouse vata; vata then hurls the sour pitta upward (udana). A spoon of goghrita is the prescription, not oil-fried food |
| Matravat — right quantity | promotes life, passes easily, does not disturb agni, digests without discomfort | Over-filling the amashaya is the mechanical root of urdhvaga movement (Ch.Vi 2/7: "all three doshas compressed and vitiated") |
| Jirne ashniyat — after the previous meal is digested | eating over undigested food "vitiates all doshas quickly"; the new food mixes with ama → shukta | The core of the Kashyapa samprapti; signs of digestion listed in §4 |
| Virya-aviruddham — non-antagonistic | avoids diseases born of unsuitable food | §5 |
| Ishte deshe, ishta sarvopakaranam — pleasant place and utensils | prevents disturbance of mind; food "not appreciated" is not digested | Manasika nidana of Amlapitta are chinta, shoka, bhaya, krodha (Ch.Vi 2/9; KS) — the table matters |
| Na atidrutam — not too fast | food goes the wrong way (utsnehana, apratishthana), taste not perceived | Fast eating swallows air (vata) and food enters the kantha again as udgara; chew until the food is liquid |
| Na ativilambitam — not too slow | food cools, becomes vishama-paka, satisfaction fails, more is eaten | Cold half-eaten meals congeal kapha; keep a warm, steady pace |
| Ajalpan, ahasan, tanmana bhunjita — no talking, no laughing, attentive | same faults as too fast; mind elsewhere → dosha vitiation | For a kantha disease this is the single most useful rule: talking while eating drives udana vayu against the food stream and opens the gate to the throat; silence during meals also reduces post-meal throat-clearing |
| Atmanam abhisamikshya — knowing oneself | what is suitable to me, what is not | Keep a personal upashaya/anupashaya diary (Kashyapa gives: pittaja Amlapitta eases with svadu-sheeta; kaphaja with ruksha-ushna) |

AH.Su 8 adds (via T4 ahsutrasthana.blogspot / easyayurveda): eat alone or in quiet, all six tastes present but madhura predominant, followed by a suitable anupana [verified x2].

## 4. Matra, timing, position, anupana

### 4a. Quantity — Trividha Kukshiya (Ch.Vi 2/3-7) [verified x2: T1 carakasamhitaonline; T4 easyayurveda]

- Divide the kukshi into three: one part solid food (murta ahara), one part liquid (drava), one part left for vata-pitta-kapha to move. AH.Su 8 phrases the same as "two parts solid, one liquid, one empty" when solid includes the wet cooked dish — the intent is identical: never fill the third part. [variant in phrasing, same rule]
- Ch.Su 5/6-7: guru foods to one-third or one-half of satiety; laghu foods not to full satiety either, "to protect agni".
- Signs of matravat ahara (Ch.Vi 2/6): no pressure in the belly, no obstruction at the heart (hridaya-anavarodha), no pain in the flanks, hunger and thirst both settled, standing/sitting/walking/breathing/laughing/TALKING all easy, morning meal digested by evening and evening by morning, bala-varna-pushti gained. "Hridaya anavarodha" and easy speech are the Amlapitta patient's daily test: if the chest feels blocked or the voice is thick after a meal, the matra was wrong.
- Signs of ati-matra (Ch.Vi 2/7): all three doshas vitiated at once — vata: shula, anaha; pitta: jvara, trishna, daha, bhrama; kapha: chardi, hrillasa, praseka, gaurava, alasya. Then ama forms and "moves up or down" (urdhva-adho-gati) — the direct classical image of reflux to the throat.
- Ama arises even from a right quantity when (Ch.Vi 2/8-9): the food is guru, sheeta, ruksha, shushka, dvishta (disliked), vishtambhi, vidahi, ashuchi, viruddha; when eaten in grief, fear, anger, sorrow; after excessive water; after vega-dharana; with disturbed sleep or night-waking [verified x2].

### 4b. Number and timing of meals

- Dvikala bhojana: the classical norm is two principal meals, morning (after the night's food is digested) and evening; Su.Su 46 and AH.Su 8 speak of "kale bhojana" — at the fixed time, once digestion is complete [verified x2 as principle; exact verse numbers not confirmed in fetched sources].
- Eat only when (AH.Su 8/55, Sanskrit read 2026-09-18: "prasrishte vinmutre hridi suvimale doshe svapathage vishuddhe chodgare kshudupagamane vate anusarati | tatha agnav udrikte vishada-karane dehe cha sulaghau prayunjita aharam… kalah sa hi matah"): mala-mutra passed, mind clear, doshas in their own paths, udgara shuddha (clean belching — no sour or food-smelling belch), kshut manifest, adho-vata moving, agni felt, indriyas clear, body light [verified x2: T1 AH.Su 8/55; T4 easyayurveda]. For Amlapitta the "clean belch" sign is the gate: sour belching = do not eat, take warm water and wait.
- Sayam bhojana: dinner in the first prahara (first three hours) of night, lighter than the midday meal (T4 easyayurveda Ratricharya; Su.Chi 24/75 "do not eat past the appointed time") [verified x2 as principle]. Practical reading for Urdhvaga Amlapitta: eat before or near sunset, so that the meal is well past the amashaya before lying down. The interval to sleep should be long enough that the "clean belch / lightness" signs return — commonly two to three hours in practitioner tradition (T4) [single-source at T4].
- Nishi (night) prohibitions: dadhi never at night (Ch.Su 7/61-62; AH.Su 5/30); saktu not at night (AH.Su 6/38-39); guru, vidahi, tila, masha, sour fruit, fried food — all worst at night when kapha rules and agni is low [verified x2].
- Divaswapna: no day sleep after meals (KS.Khila 16 nidana; AH.Su 3/54 for Sharad; AH.Su 7) — sleep after eating raises kapha and mandagni and lets pitta-kapha rise to the kantha [verified x2].

### 4c. Post-meal conduct and the left side

- Su.Su 46 (post-meal section; read in the Bhishagratna translation 2026-09-18: "Then the eater should take rest, like a king, till the sense of drowsiness incidental to eating is removed. After this he should walk a hundred paces and lie down in a bed on his left side."), corroborated T4 easyayurveda Ratricharya: sit at ease "like a king" until the heaviness of food settles; then walk a hundred steps (shatapada); then lie down on the LEFT side (vama-parshva) for a short rest. [verified x2: T1 Su.Su 46 primary text; T4 S24 — the translation carries no verse number for this passage, so the verse number remains unconfirmed]. The left flank is named among the sites related to the annavaha srotas; lying on the left is understood to settle the food in the amashaya and let samana vayu work.
- Interaction with Urdhvaga Amlapitta: the classical vama-kukshi rest is a SHORT rest after lunch (not sleep, not lying flat, not after dinner). For a patient whose pitta rises to the kantha, the practitioner reading (T4 IJNRD 2023 review echoes: "avoid supine immediately after eating; left lateral is the advised position") is: sit upright until lightness returns, walk 100 steps, and if resting, the left side with the head raised; never the right side, never supine, never within the first hour of a meal. At night, do not lie down until the udgara is clean [verified x2 at principle level].
- Snana / avagahana immediately after meals is a Kashyapa nidana (KS.Khila 16/6 "bhuktva yuktva divasvapnad ati-snanavagahanat") [verified x2: T1 KS.Khila 16/6 (primary, 2026-09-18); T3 IJAM 2016]. Su.Su 46 agrees: after a full meal, avoid long siesta, fire, sun, travel, driving, swimming and bathing.
- After eating or drinking, avoid speaking, long walking, sleep, sun, fire, travel in vehicles, swimming and riding — AH.Su 8/54 (Sanskrit read 2026-09-18: "sarvashcha bhashyam adhvashanam tyajet pitva bhuktva atapam vahnim yanam plavana vahanam") and Su.Su 46 (post-meal section, Bhishagratna) [verified x2: T1 AH.Su 8/54; T1 Su.Su 46]. CORRECTED 2026-09-18: this list is NOT in Ch.Su 27/327-328 — that verse pair forbids the after-drink itself (below), not post-meal conduct. For a voice-using patient: no long calls or lectures straight after meals.

### 4d. Anupana (after-drink) — Su.Su 46/446-447, Ch.Su 27/319-328, AH.Su 8/42-54 [verified x2: T3 IJAPR 2021; T4 easyayurveda AH 8]

- Definition: the drink taken after (or with) food; best anupana is water; anupana should not share the food's qualities nor oppose the dhatus.
- Which drink after what (Sushruta/Vagbhata): cold (boiled-cooled) water after yava, godhuma, madhu, dadhi; warm water after pishtanna (starchy, doughy foods) and after shaka dishes; ushna jala after ghrita and any sneha; milk for the weak, aged, convalescent and for those exhausted by talking, walking, sun or wind (T3 IJAPR table 9: adhika bhashya → dugdha) — relevant to a voice-strained patient; yusha after taila; meat soup for dhatu-kshaya.
- Hot vs cold for Amlapitta: sheeta jala relieves daha, trishna, chardi, murcha, bhrama, pitta (AH.Su 5/15) — but the classical lists specify TAPTA-SHEETA (boiled then cooled), never raw cold; ushnodaka is deepana, pachana, kanthya, relieves hikka, kasa, ama, adhmana (AH.Su 5/16-17). Working rule: warm sips during and just after meals; boiled-cooled water between meals; ice-cold never [verified x2].
- Timing effect (AH.Su 8 / BP Purvakhanda, the "bhojanadau jalam" rule): water before food → karshya (thins, weakens agni); after food → sthaulya and kapha; in the middle (small amounts between mouthfuls) → sama, balanced. Kashyapa counts excess water "in between food" as agnimandya-kara. Therefore: small sips only; no glassfuls before, with or immediately after; volume drinking 45-60 minutes after the meal in practitioner tradition (T4) [verified x2 for classical rule; T4 for the interval].
- ANUPANA CONTRAINDICATIONS — the two verse-sets read in the primary on 2026-09-18, and they differ, so both are given:
  - Ch.Su 27/327-328 (Sanskrit confirmed): "nordhvangamarutavishta na hikka-shvasa-kasinah | na gita-bhashyadhyayana-prasakta norasi kshatah || pibeyur udakam bhuktva tad dhi kantha-urasi sthitam | sneham aharajam hatva bhuyo doshaya kalpate ||" — those with vata provoked in the UPPER PARTS of the body, hikka, shvasa, kasa, urah-kshata, and those engaged in singing, lecturing or study, must not drink water after eating. Charaka gives his OWN reason, which is the one to quote: the drink STAYS IN THE THROAT AND CHEST (kantha-urasi sthitam), destroys the unctuousness the meal gave them, and thereby breeds greater dosha.
  - AH.Su 8/53 (Sanskrit confirmed): "nordhvajatrugada-shvasa-kasorah-kshata-pinase | gita-bhashya-prasange cha svarabhede cha tad-dhitam" — adds urdhva-jatru-gata roga (all disease above the collarbone: kantha, mukha, nasa, akshi, shiras), pinasa and SVARABHEDA (hoarseness), which Charaka does not name. AH.Su 8 continues immediately: "praklinna-deha-meha-akshi-GALAROGA-vranaturah panam tyajeyuh" — those with a waterlogged body, meha, eye disease, THROAT DISEASE (gala-roga) or ulcers should give up drinks altogether.
  [CORRECTED 2026-09-18: the composite list previously carried here was AH.Su 8/53's, not Charaka's; Su.Su 46/446-447 as a verse number rests on the S18 table only and is left [single-source at T3].] This is the classical rule most directly written for the kantha-gata (throat-seated) Urdhvaga Amlapitta picture: a hoarse, throat-clearing patient should NOT wash meals down. Bhojanante madhu: honey is named as anupana for the sthula and for kapha — for Amlapitta it is only pathya in cooled water or with laja saktu, never in anything hot (AH.Su 5/53) [verified x2].

## 5. Viruddha Ahara (Ch.Su 26/81-106) with everyday examples [verified x2: T1 carakasamhitaonline; T4 easyayurveda; T3 PMC "Viruddha Ahara: a critical view"]

Definition (Ch.Su 26/81, 85): that which vitiates the doshas but does not expel them from the body is viruddha — the doshas thus roused sit in the dhatus and srotas; amlapitta is named among the resulting diseases (Ch.Su 26/102-103: kushtha, visarpa, unmada, andhata, grahani, amlapitta, jvara, pinasa, shopha, murcha, death).

## 6. Takra (buttermilk) doctrine

### 6a. What takra is — types by water and fat (Su.Su 45; BP Purvakhanda; Sharangadhara; Bhojanakutuhala) [verified x2: T4 easyayurveda 2010; T3 IJAAS 2016; T3 Cuest. Fisioter. 2025]

| Name | Preparation | Property |
|---|---|---|
| Ghola | curd churned, no water added, cream kept | vata-pitta hara, heavier |
| Mathita | churned, cream removed, no water | kapha-pitta hara, lighter |
| Takra | churned with one-fourth water (Su.Su 45 / BP: "ardhodaka" = half water in some readings) [variant: 1/4 vs 1/2 water] | the standard therapeutic form: laghu, kashaya-amla, deepana, kapha-vata hara |
| Udashvit | half curd, half water | pitta-kapha hara, deepana |
| Chhachchhika | much water, cream removed ("svaccha prachura varika") | sheeta, laghu, pitta-shrama-trishna hara, vata-nut, kapha-krit; deepana only if saindhava is added (Bhojanakutuhala, quoted T3 Cuest. Fisioter. 2025) |

Fat grades (BP; T3 IJAAS): ruksha takra (all fat removed), ardha-uddhrita-sneha (half fat removed), sneha-yukta (fat kept).

### 6b. Properties and praise

- AH.Su 5/33-34: "takram laghu kashaya-amlam deepanam kapha-vata-jit" — light, astringent-sour, kindles agni, subdues kapha and vata; indicated in shopha, arsha, grahani-dosha, mutragraha, udara, aruchi, pliha, ghrita-vyapat (ghee indigestion), gara, pandu [verified x2: T4 easyayurveda AH 5; T4 ayurvedahub].
- Ch.Chi 15/117-119 (Sanskrit read in the primary 2026-09-18): "takram tu grahanidoshe dipana-grahi-laghavat || shreshtham madhurapakitvan na cha pittam prakopayet | kashayoshna-vikashitvad raukshyach chaiva kaphe hitam || vate svadv-amla-sandratvat sadyaskam avidahi tat" — takra is best in grahani-dosha for its deepana, grahi and laghu action; it is MADHURA IN VIPAKA and therefore does not aggravate pitta; it is kashaya, USHNA in virya, vikasi and ruksha and therefore good in kapha; sweet-sour and sandra, it settles vata; and FRESHLY CHURNED (sadyaska) takra is AVIDAHI. [verified x2: T1 carakasamhitaonline primary; T3 IJAAS]. CORRECTED: the text attributes the non-aggravation of pitta to madhura vipaka alone, not to "non-excess of ushna" — the verse plainly calls takra ushna in virya. The line "diseases cured by takra do not recur" is not in 117-119; it belongs with the Bhavaprakasha praise below [single-source].
- Bhavaprakasha praise: "takram shakrasya durlabham" — buttermilk is rare even for Indra; "as amrita for gods, takra for men" (T4 ayurvedahub; T4 easyayurveda) [single-source lineage, widely quoted].
- Praseka (excess salivation/throat mucus) and aruchi are in the indication list (Ch.Su 27; Cuest. list) — takra thins and clears kledaka kapha from the amashaya, which is the kapha component of Urdhvaga Amlapitta.

### 6c. Contraindications and the Amlapitta tension [variant — recorded fully]

- Su.Su 45 (Bhishagratna translation read 2026-09-18, verbatim): "The use of Takra is prohibited in the hot season, nor should it be given to a weak person, nor to one suffering from an ulcer, or laid up with an attack of hemoptysis, or to one suffering from epileptic fits, vertigo (Bhrama), or from a burning sensation in the body. The use of Takra is recommended during the cold months of the year." So: grishma/ushna kala, daurbalya, kshata, raktapitta, murcha, BHRAMA and DAHA. BP Purvakhanda / T3 lists add sharad [verified x2: T1 Su.Su 45 primary; T3 Cuest. 2025; T4 easyayurveda]. CORRECTED 2026-09-18: "heat-making in potency" in Bhishagratna's Su.Su 45 is said of DADHI, not of takra; takra's ushna virya is stated by Ch.Chi 15/118 above.
- Daha, bhrama and murcha are themselves Amlapitta lakshanas (MN 51). So: takra is NOT a first-line drink in the acute burning phase of Urdhvaga Amlapitta; none of the four Amlapitta pathya lists (Kashyapa, YR, BP, BR) names takra. Takra enters when the disease is kapha-dominant (gaurava, praseka, aruchi, utklesha without daha) or has settled into grahani-type mandagni [variant: T4 practitioner tradition often prescribes takra freely for "acidity"; the classical lists do not].
- Kantha and takra — GAP PARTLY CLOSED 2026-09-18. No verse naming takra and kantha roga together was found; but a classical basis for the caution now exists and it is stronger than the anupana rule alone: (i) AH.Su 8, immediately after the anupana contraindication of 8/53, states "praklinna-deha-meha-akshi-GALAROGA-vranaturah panam tyajeyuh" — one with THROAT DISEASE should give up pana (drinks) altogether; takra taken as a drink falls squarely under pana. (ii) Ch.Su 27/327-328 bars the after-drink for those with vata provoked above, kasa, shvasa, urah-kshata and for heavy speakers, with the reason that the drink lodges in kantha and ura. (iii) AH.Su 8/53 adds urdhva-jatru-gata roga and svarabheda. (iv) Takra is kashaya-AMLA and ushna in virya (Ch.Chi 15/118), and amla ati-sevana "paridahati kantham" (Ch.Su 26/43), while cold, sour, abhishyandi drinks raise praseka and kantha-kandu (AH.Su 5/29-32 for dadhi). Together these make takra-as-a-drink apathya for an active kantha-gata Amlapitta. Fresh, thin, room-temperature, pitta-mode takra taken WITH the midday meal (not as a wash-down) is the only admissible form. [variant; no verse names takra in kantha roga — discuss with your vaidya.]

### 6d. How to prepare and take (when appropriate)

- Fresh curd set that morning from boiled cow's milk (never day-old sour curd — sour curd = amla vipaka, pitta-kara); churn with water (1:4 to 1:2), remove the butter that rises; the residue is takra (T4 easyayurveda 2010; T3 IJAAS) [verified x2].
- Dosha-wise additions (T3 Cuest. 2025 citing BP): pitta-dominant → sharkara/sita and a little jeeraka, dhanyaka (madhura-sheeta); kapha-dominant → shunthi, a pinch of saindhava, jeeraka, roasted hingu (small); vata-dominant → saindhava, shunthi. For Urdhvaga Amlapitta: pitta-mode additions, room temperature, midday only.
- Time: with or after the midday meal, never at night, never in Sharad/Grishma heat, never on a day with daha or sour belching; a small cup (about 100-150 ml practitioner measure, T4) [single-source at T4 for quantity].
- Stop if praseka, kantha-kandu or daha increase.

### 7b. Sample day (sama-agni, non-acute phase; adjust by §8-9)

- On waking: ushnodaka 1 cup in sips; then elimination; no food until udgara shuddha and kshut are present (AH.Su 8/55).
- Pratah bhojana (breakfast, after sunrise, light): OPTION A yava or shali peya/yavagu cooked soft in water with a little milk, pinch of ela, tsp goghrita; OPTION B laja with warm milk and sita; OPTION C soaked draksha + a small bowl of cooked dalia. No curd, no fruit-with-milk, no coffee/tea. (T4 offers a satmya concession — weak tea after food, never with milk plus a sour snack — but Ch.Su 26/104-106 is explicit that satmya, small quantity, strong agni and youth only SOFTEN the harm of an unsuitable article; they do not make it pathya. Treat the concession as a taper, not a permission) [variant at T4].
- Mid-morning: narikela jala, or dhanyaka-jeeraka water.
- Madhyahna bhojana (main meal, when the sun is highest — agni strongest, AH.Su 3): purana shali rice soft-cooked; mudga yusha with dhanyaka-jeeraka-haridra, tsp ghee, saindhava minimal; a tikta vegetable (patola / karavella / karkotaka) cooked with a little goghrita (not taila — sesame oil is ushna and pitta-kara, §2d); kushmanda or lauki sabzi; a small thin godhuma roti if desired; a spoon of dadima seeds or amalaki murabba as the "madhura first / kashaya last" bracket; sips of warm water; takra (pitta-mode, §6) only if kapha-dominant and no daha. Then sit "like a king", walk 100 steps, short left-side rest with head raised (§4c).
- Aparahna: no snacking; if needed, soaked draksha or a sweet ripe fruit alone; ushira water.
- Sayam bhojana (before/around sunset, half the size of lunch): manda/peya of old rice or yava with ghee; or mudga yusha with laja; or dalia. No dadhi, no tila, no fried, no sour fruit, no cheese. Warm sips only; no after-drink volume (anupana rule for kantha roga).
- After dinner: upright at least until lightness returns; 100 steps; no day-sleep-type lying; sleep once udgara is clean; head of the bed higher than the feet is practitioner tradition (T4) [single-source].
- Before sleep (only if hungry and kapha-free): half cup boiled warm cow's milk with ela — never if praseka/kantha-kapha present that day.

### 7c. Langhana / ama-pachana day menu (see §9)

Manda → peya → vilepi → yusha ladder (Ch.Vi 2/13 "viriktavat anupurvi" — in ama the sequence followed after virechana; AH.Su 6/26-31 for the properties of each step. CORRECTED 2026-09-18 from "Ch.Vi 2/10-12", which is visuchika and alasaka, not the ladder): thin rice water (manda) morning; peya (thin gruel, 1:14 grain:water) midday with jeeraka and saindhava; vilepi (thicker) evening only if hunger is clean; hot water throughout; dhanyaka-jeeraka water; no milk, no ghee, no takra until nishthiva/gaurava/aruchi clear.

### 7d. Kantha-specific foods

- Kantha-hitakara / Kanthya — Ch.Su 4, the ninth mahakashaya, read in the primary 2026-09-18: "sariveksumula-madhuka-pippali-draksha-vidari-kaitarya-hamsapadi-brihati-kantakarika iti dashemani kanthyani bhavanti (9)". Charaka's own commentary on the gana adds that throat disorders are tridoshaja with VATA-KAPHA predominance, so vata-kapha-pacifying drugs are the kanthya ones — which is why the Amlapitta plate must not be made ati-ruksha or ati-sheeta in chasing pitta. Madhuka (yashtimadhu) is separately the agrya kanthya: "madhukam chakshushya-vrishya-keshya-KANTHYA-varnya…" (Ch.Su 25/40). Pippali is in the gana but is ushna and is not taken here as a food (see the Kashyapa bala-clause [variant], §1 note). [verified x2: T1 Ch.Su 4 mahakashaya 9 and Ch.Su 25/40, primary; T4 easyayurveda; T3 JAIMS]. Dietary members: yashtimadhu (as a small piece chewed or in warm — not hot — water), draksha (soaked raisins chewed), ikshu/sita (rock sugar dissolved slowly), ghrita (AH.Su 5/37-39; Su.Su 45 "improves voice"), boiled milk. Ushnodaka itself is "kanthya" (AH.Su 5/16-17). Madhu in TAPTA-SHEETA (boiled-and-cooled) water or with laja saktu is kapha-hara for a mucus-coated kantha (AH.Su 5/51-53) — never warm, never hot, never in daha. [CORRECTED 2026-09-18: this line read "with warm water", contradicting §1 ("never heated, never with heat"), §4d and §7a ("only cooled liquids").]
- Kantha-ahitakara (foods that cause praseka, kantha-kandu, svara-bheda): dadhi (abhishyandi, kapha-pitta) and all cold thick dairy; sheeta pana (cold drinks — congeal kapha; Su.Su 45: cold water avoided in "catarrh"); guru-abhishyandi foods (tila, navanna, fried, cheese); amla excess ("burns the kantha" Ch.Su 26/43); katu excess (kantha-shosha); kashaya excess (vak-graha — thick speech); unripe kapittha (kantha-ahita, AH.Su 6/125); large after-drinks (anupana rule); saktu at night; talking during meals [verified x2].

- Ritusandhi (AH.Su 3/58): change regimen gradually over the last 7 days of one season and first 7 of the next; sudden change breeds disease — the Amlapitta patient who flips from winter's ghee-milk plate to spring's dry plate in a day gets vishamagni.
- Desha correction for a patient outside India (Ch.Ka 1/8 desha types; Kashyapa's anupa note) [verified x2 for desha classification]: cold-dry temperate climates are jangala-like — vata-pitta; the vata share of Amlapitta rises (dryness of kantha, udgara, vishamagni, hoarseness from ruksha). Kashyapa's sequence applies: jangala-grown dravyas and a jangala-type plate first, deshantara only if the disease will not settle (KS.Khila 16/44-45). Corrections: keep snigdha (ghee, boiled milk) even while keeping sheeta-tikta; never ati-ruksha (no dry cracker/salad/toast diets); warm food always; indoor-heated dry air = extra ruksha, so warm water sips through the day; the Hemanta-type regimen applies for more months than the Indian calendar suggests, and Sharad-type strictness applies whenever the local autumn is warm and sunny. Damp, cool, maritime places behave as anupa — kapha-vata: takra (kapha-mode), yava, laja, tikta greens, lighter dairy. Map your local six-season equivalent with your vaidya.

## 9. Agni assessment and Ama — how the diet changes

### 9a. The four agni (Ch.Chi 15/50-51; AH.Su 12) [verified x2: T1 carakasamhitaonline; T4]

| Agni | Dosha | Signs | Diet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vishama | Vata | digests sometimes well, sometimes not; adhmana, udgara, shula, irregular hunger; hoarseness from ruksha | regular times; warm, snigdha (ghee), soft, madhura-amla(amalaki)-lavana(minimal saindhava) — Ch.Su rule for vata; avoid ruksha, sheeta, fasting; fennel-cumin water |
| Tikshna | Pitta | burning hunger, digests even much food, daha, trishna, sour-bitter belching when empty, tikta mouth | never skip meals; madhura-sheeta-snigdha in fair quantity: milk, ghee, rice, wheat, sita, draksha; tikta greens; no fasting-langhana; no katu-amla-lavana; "Amlapitta with tikshnagni" is the pitta subtype (KS.Khila 16/16: svadu-sheeta upashaya) |
| Manda | Kapha | heaviness, aruchi, praseka, utklesha, nausea, sleepy after food, food sits for hours | laghu, ruksha-leaning, ushna-cooked; yava, laja, mudga yusha, tikta; shunthi pinch; takra (kapha-mode); small meals, longer gaps; no milk at night, no ghee excess (KS.Khila 16/17: kaphaja Amlapitta — ruksha-ushna upashaya) |
| Sama | balanced | clean belch, timely hunger, light body | the §7b day |

### 9b. Ama and Sama-dosha signs (AH.Su 13/23-26) [verified x2: T4 easyayurveda AH 13; T4 pocketayurveda]

- Definition (AH.Su 13/25): from weak ushma (agni) the first dhatu (rasa) remains undigested — that is ama, which lodges in the amashaya; (13/26) or, ama is the mutual mixing of greatly increased doshas, "like poison from kodrava grain".
- Sama-dosha signs (AH.Su 13/23-24): srotorodha (blocked channels — feeling of a lump, thick throat), bala-bhramsha (loss of strength), gaurava (heaviness), anila-mudhata (vata not moving — bloating, no clean belch), alasya, apakti (indigestion), nishthiva (repeated spitting/throat-clearing of sticky saliva), mala-sanga (stool held), aruchi, klama (tiredness without work). Nishthiva and srotorodha are the direct ama-face of a throat-clearing patient; when present, the day is an ama day.
- Treatment order (AH.Su 13/29): pachana and deepana first, then snehana-svedana, then shodhana. Ch.Vi 2/13 (Sanskrit read 2026-09-18): in visuchika, langhana first and then the order followed after virechana; in ama-pradosha give pachana and deepana and NO FOOD during ajirna, "for agni already weakened by ama cannot digest dosha, drug and food at once"; the diseases born of ama are settled by apatarpana; if the ama is of the alasaka type and still in the amashaya — vamana (vaidya-prescribed) after warm saline water; then the regimen "as after virechana" (viriktavat anupurvi) — the manda-peya-vilepi-yusha ladder.

### 9c. Ama-pachana diet days (langhana)

- Langhana here = laghu bhojana, not starvation (Kashyapa: langhana and laghu bhojana after shodhana; tikshnagni and vata patients must not fast).
- Day plan: ushnodaka on waking and hourly in sips; manda (rice water) mid-morning; peya at midday with jeeraka, dhanyaka, a pinch of saindhava; dhanyaka-jeeraka siddha jala through the afternoon (T4 easyayurveda; T3 IJNRD lists coriander-seed water) [verified x2 at T3/T4]; vilepi or thin mudga yusha in the evening if hunger is clean; no milk, ghee, takra, fruit, sweets until the sama signs lift.
- Return signs (nirama): udgara shuddha, laghuta, real hunger, thin clear saliva, tongue uncoated, stool passes (AH.Su 8/55).
- When ama returns to sama, reintroduce ghee before milk, milk before takra, fruit last.
